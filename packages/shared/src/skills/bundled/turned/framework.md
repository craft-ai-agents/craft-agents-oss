# TURNED — Tactical Frameworks

Reference document for the operational playbooks used by the `turned` skill.

## 1. Archetype Fusion Matrix

The most seductive brands rarely embody a single archetype. They **fuse** two or more, creating dimensional depth that resists categorization. The fusion must be coherent — the archetypes must amplify each other, not compete.

### High-Potential Fusions
| Fusion | Mechanism | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Star + Coquette** | Glamorous presence + strategic unavailability | Supreme, Hermès |
| **Charismatic + Dandy** | Visionary leadership + radical nonconformity | Tesla/Musk, Vivienne Westwood |
| **Siren + Rake** | Sensual overload + obsessive devotion | Rihanna/Fenty, certain luxury hospitality |
| **Ideal Lover + Natural** | Mirrored fantasy + innocent spontaneity | Peloton (early), certain wellness brands |
| **Charmer + Machiavellian** | Mirrored desire + strategic manipulation | TikTok, Amazon |
| **Coquette + Psychopathy** | Hot/cold dynamics + emotional coldness | Balenciaga, certain meme brands |

### Fusion Incompatibility
Some archetypes cancel each other out:
- **Natural + Narcissist:** The innocent cannot simultaneously be the self-obsessed protagonist. One must dominate.
- **Charmer + Dandy:** The Charmer reflects others; the Dandy reflects only themselves. The Dandy's self-focus destroys the Charmer's mirroring.
- **Ideal Lover + Coquette:** The Ideal Lover gives completely; the Coquette withholds strategically. Fusion requires one to be surface, one to be depth.

### Archetype Calibration Guide
When auditing a brand, score each archetype 0-100:

| Archetype | Score | Evidence |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Siren | ___ | Sensory presence, ungraspable promise, danger |
| Rake | ___ | Obsessive devotion, linguistic seduction, recklessness |
| Ideal Lover | ___ | Mirrored fantasy, observation, idealization |
| Dandy | ___ | Nonconformity, radical difference, impudence |
| Natural | ___ | Innocence, spontaneity, vulnerability |
| Coquette | ___ | Hot/cold dynamics, unavailability, strategic withdrawal |
| Charmer | ___ | Flattery, mirroring, complete attention |
| Charismatic | ___ | Vision, contradiction, self-sufficiency |
| Star | ___ | Glamour, drama, mythological presence |

**Dominant Archetype:** The highest score.
**Shadow Archetype:** The second-highest score that could be amplified.
**Fusion Potential:** If two scores are within 15 points of each other, fusion is natural. If one dominates by >30 points, the brand is one-dimensional.

## 2. Dark Triad Calibration Guide

The Dark Triad is not about being evil. It is about being **effective in attention economies that reward polarity**. Each trait has optimal deployment levels and dangerous excess.

### Narcissism Calibration
| Score | Effect | Risk |
|-------|--------|------|
| 0-20 | Invisible. The brand apologizes for existing. | No attention, no identity. |
| 21-40 | Humble. The brand serves users quietly. | Preference, not possession. |
| 41-60 | Confident. The brand knows its value. | Solid, forgettable. |
| 61-80 | Commanding. The brand is the protagonist. | **Optimal zone.** Attention magnet. |
| 81-95 | Messianic. The brand is the meaning. | High possession, high backlash risk. |
| 96-100 | Delusional. The brand believes its own mythology to the point of functional failure. | Cult collapse, mass exodus when reality intrudes. |

**Narcissism Tactics:**
- Self-mythology through origin stories
- Founder as protagonist (not servant)
- Vision statements that center the brand's destiny
- Product launches as cultural events, not commercial announcements
- Refusal to apologize for ambition
- Visual self-aggrandizement (monumental branding, monumental product photography)

### Machiavellianism Calibration
| Score | Effect | Risk |
|-------|--------|------|
| 0-20 | Transparent. No strategy visible. | Competitors copy instantly. |
| 21-40 | Tactful. Some strategic thinking. | Reactive, not predictive. |
| 41-60 | Strategic. Clear playbook. | Predictable to sophisticated observers. |
| 61-80 | Manipulative. Invisible architecture guides behavior. | **Optimal zone.** Users follow the path without seeing it. |
| 81-95 | Orchestral. Every user action is composed. | Paranoia if exposed. Users feel puppeteered. |
| 96-100 | Parasitic. The brand extracts value while destroying user trust. | Short-term gain, long-term collapse. |

**Machiavellianism Tactics:**
- Social proof engineering ("X people are viewing this now")
- Artificial scarcity with invisible replenishment logic
- Information asymmetry (insider communities, "secret" knowledge)
- Proxy armies (organic-seeming advocates who are actually deployed)
- Incentive manipulation (rewarding behaviors that serve the brand's growth)
- Calculated betrayal (abandoning early adopters to reach mass market)

### Psychopathy Calibration
| Score | Effect | Risk |
|-------|--------|------|
| 0-20 | Safe. No transgression. | Wallpaper. No edge. |
| 21-40 | Playful. Mild risk-taking. | Safe enough to ignore. |
| 41-60 | Edgy. Occasional transgression. | Memorable moments, but not identity. |
| 61-80 | Dangerous. Deliberate norm violation. | **Optimal zone.** Fascination without legal/fatal risk. |
| 81-95 | Reckless. Frequent transgression with cold indifference. | High attention, high regulatory/cultural backlash. |
| 96-100 | Destructive. The brand damages users, partners, or society for thrills. | Legal destruction, moral collapse, mass exodus. |

**Psychopathy Tactics:**
- Deliberate norm violation (saying what "shouldn't" be said)
- Emotional coldness in messaging (no warmth, no apology)
- Trolling as marketing (provoking response through calculated offense)
- Risk-taking that terrifies conventional competitors
- Indifference to criticism ("we do what we want")
- Transgressive product/content that challenges taboos

### Triad Balance
The most potent brands balance all three traits:
- **Narcissism** provides the magnetism ("Look at me")
- **Machiavellianism** provides the architecture ("Follow this path")
- **Psychopathy** provides the edge ("We don't care what you think")

An unbalanced brand:
- **High Narcissism + Low Machiavellianism:** Magnetic but directionless. Users gather but don't act.
- **High Machiavellianism + Low Narcissism:** Effective but invisible. Users follow the path but don't love the guide.
- **High Psychopathy + Low Narcissism:** Edgy but forgettable. Users are shocked but not seduced.

## 3. Cult Mechanics Implementation Roadmap

The six indoctrination mechanics can be implemented sequentially or simultaneously. The roadmap below shows the optimal sequence for building possession from scratch.

### Phase 1: Love-Bombing (Weeks 1-4)
**Goal:** Create an emotional high that users spend the rest of their relationship trying to recapture.

**Tactics:**
- Spectacular onboarding sequence (not just functional — emotional)
- Personal welcome from founder/community leader (real or carefully simulated)
- Immediate validation: "You made the right choice" (not "thank you for purchasing")
- Gift with first purchase (unexpected, not advertised)
- Early access to something exclusive
- Community welcome that feels like joining a family

**Metrics:**
- Onboarding completion rate (target: >90%)
- First-week emotional response (survey: "How did joining make you feel?" Target: >70% "excited/thrilled")
- Repeat engagement within 48 hours (target: >60%)

**Taper Strategy:**
Week 1-2: Daily attention
Week 3-4: Every other day
Week 5+: Weekly strategic touches
The taper creates hunger. The hunger creates craving.

### Phase 2: Isolation (Weeks 4-12)
**Goal:** The brand ecosystem becomes the user's primary source of validation, information, and identity.

**Tactics:**
- Proprietary communication channels (app-only messaging, brand-owned community)
- Unique vocabulary/language that makes external communication feel incomplete
- Content that is only available inside the ecosystem
- Tools that replace external alternatives (calendar, notes, social — all inside the brand)
- Rituals that require brand participation to be meaningful

**Metrics:**
- % of user's digital time spent in ecosystem (target: >40% for power users)
- External tool replacement rate (target: 3+ external tools replaced by brand tools)
- Community participation rate (target: >30% of users participate in brand-owned community weekly)

### Phase 3: Ritual (Weeks 8-16)
**Goal:** Repetitive rituals recondition users to associate the brand with meaning and transcendence.

**Tactics:**
- Daily ritual design (morning coffee with the app, evening wind-down with content)
- Weekly community events (predictable, branded, emotionally meaningful)
- Seasonal/cyclical rituals (drops, launches, celebrations, anniversaries)
- Physical rituals (unboxing as ceremony, wearing brand colors on specific days)
- Social rituals (sharing achievements in brand-specific formats, using brand hashtags)

**Metrics:**
- Daily active ritual participation (target: >25% of user base)
- Weekly event attendance (target: >15%)
- Ritual-associated emotional response (target: >60% report "meaningful" or "important")

### Phase 4: Us-vs-Them (Weeks 12-24)
**Goal:** Tribal identity that makes criticism of the brand feel like personal attack.

**Tactics:**
- Enemy construction (a competitor, a technology, a cultural norm, "the mainstream")
- Insider terminology that outsiders don't understand
- Community in-jokes and references
- Narrative positioning as rebellion against corrupt/complacent mainstream
- "We're not for everyone" messaging (creates belonging through exclusion)

**Metrics:**
- Defensive response rate to brand criticism (target: >30% of community defends unprompted)
- Us-vs-them language usage in user-generated content (target: >20% of community posts)
- Exclusion-based belonging signals (target: users use "we" when referring to brand community)

### Phase 5: Confession & Vulnerability (Weeks 16-32)
**Goal:** Emotional debt through shared vulnerability. The brand becomes the repository of the user's secret self.

**Tactics:**
- Transformation narratives that require user vulnerability (before/after, struggle stories)
- Founder/leader vulnerability (failures, doubts, "human moments")
- Community spaces designed for confession (anonymous or semi-anonymous sharing)
- "Share your story" campaigns that reward vulnerability with visibility/validation
- Products that solve shame-based problems (debt, weight, addiction, loneliness)

**Metrics:**
- Vulnerability-sharing rate (target: >10% of users share personal story within first year)
- Community intimacy score (survey: "How close do you feel to other community members?" Target: >50% "very close")
- Emotional debt indicator (target: users report they "owe" the brand for helping them)

### Phase 6: Leader Mythology (Weeks 24-ongoing)
**Goal:** The leader becomes inseparable from the brand. Criticism of the leader is heresy.

**Tactics:**
- Origin story mythology (struggle, vision, messianic arrival, "the moment everything changed")
- Consistent visual/iconographic treatment of leader (specific clothing, poses, settings)
- Leader's personal values = brand values (fused, not aligned)
- Leader's enemies = brand enemies
- Leader visibility that implies destiny (not just "running a company" but "changing the world")
- Photographic treatment: monumental, mythic, not casual

**Metrics:**
- Leader-brand fusion score (survey: "Can you separate [Leader] from [Brand]?" Target: <20% say yes)
- Defensive loyalty to leader (target: >40% of community defends leader from criticism)
- Leader mythology retelling (target: >50% of users can recount origin story unprompted)

## 4. Craving Architecture Blueprint

### The Dopamine Loop Design
Every craving architecture must have three components: **Trigger**, **Behavior**, **Variable Reward**.

**Trigger:** The stimulus that creates desire. Must be unpredictable (variable ratio) to maximize addiction potential.
- External triggers: Push notification, email, social mention, community activity
- Internal triggers: Boredom, loneliness, anxiety, FOMO, identity uncertainty
- **Optimal trigger density:** 3-7 triggers per day for active users, 1-3 for dormant users

**Behavior:** The action the user takes in response to the trigger. Must be low-friction.
- Open app, check community, browse new content, make a purchase, share an achievement
- **Optimal behavior design:** One-tap or less. The easier the behavior, the stronger the habit.

**Variable Reward:** The unpredictable payoff that creates the craving loop.
- Social validation (likes, comments, followers — unpredictable volume)
- Content discovery (new, interesting, unexpected)
- Material reward (discount, gift, exclusive access — unpredictable timing)
- Information reward (insider knowledge, "secret" updates)
- **Critical rule:** The reward must be *variable*. Predictable rewards create satisfaction, not craving. Variable rewards create addiction.

### Validation Scarcity Implementation
**The 80/20 Rule of Validation:** 20% of users should receive 80% of the brand's visible attention. The 80% must feel they are striving for entry into the 20%.

**Tactics:**
- Public acknowledgment of top users (leaderboards, shoutouts, features)
- Tiered community access (public → member → insider → inner circle)
- Founder attention as ultimate prize (replies, follows, personal messages to select users)
- Content creation rewards (only the best user content gets featured)
- **Critical rule:** The striving must feel *achievable*, not impossible. If the 80% believe they can never enter the 20%, they quit. If they believe they *could* with effort, they persist.

### Attention Asymmetry Tactics
The brand must always appear to have more valuable things to do than attend to the user.

**Tactics:**
- Delayed response times (not instant — the brand has a life)
- Strategic absence (the founder disappears for weeks, then returns with something extraordinary)
- "We were building" narratives (the brand was absent because it was working on something world-changing)
- Limited availability (office hours, drop-in sessions, rare Q&As)
- The brand's attention must seem like a diversion from more important matters

## 5. World-Building Universe Construction Manual

### Step 1: Cosmology (The Origin Myth)
Every universe needs a creation story. How did this brand come to exist? What void did it fill? What injustice did it correct?
- **Example:** Apple's origin myth (garage, rebellion against IBM, "the crazy ones")
- **Audit action:** Can users recount the origin myth? If not, it doesn't exist.

### Step 2: Geography (The Spaces)
Where does this universe exist? Physical spaces, digital spaces, mental spaces.
- **Example:** Nike's universe includes the track, the gym, the marathon, the inner mental space of the athlete
- **Audit action:** Can users name the spaces? Do the spaces have distinct emotional qualities?

### Step 3: Inhabitants (The Characters)
Who lives in this universe? Founders, users, mascots, enemies, allies.
- **Example:** Tesla's inhabitants include Musk (visionary), the early adopters (believers), legacy auto (villains), the planet (the beneficiary)
- **Audit action:** Are the characters vivid? Can users describe them? Is there a protagonist?

### Step 4: Lore (The Stories)
The legends, myths, mysteries, and forbidden knowledge. Lore must be deep, discoverable, and never fully knowable.
- **Example:** Supreme's lore (founder mystery, drop mythology, resale economy as folklore)
- **Audit action:** Can users discover lore? Is there more to discover after years of participation?

### Step 5: Rituals (The Practices)
What do inhabitants DO in this universe? How do they enter? How do they belong? How do they advance?
- **Example:** Apple's rituals (Keynote attendance, unboxing, upgrade cycles, Genius Bar visits)
- **Audit action:** Are there entry rituals? Membership markers? Advancement rituals?

### Step 6: Destiny (The Future)
Where is this universe going? What is the ultimate purpose? What happens to inhabitants who stay?
- **Example:** Tesla's destiny (sustainable transport, multi-planetary species)
- **Audit action:** Do users feel part of a journey toward a meaningful future?

---

These frameworks are not suggestions. They are constraints. Work within them, leverage them deliberately, and cite them when auditing. The `turned` skill applies them as first-class tools, not decoration.
