# SLAY-SCRIPT — Tactical Frameworks

The playbooks slay-script runs at the line. If `SKILL.md` is the doctrine, this is the choreography.

## 1. The 5 Threshold-Science Engineering Protocols

Every hook violates at least one — strong hooks stack 2–3. If a draft can't name its violations, it isn't a hook.

### Prediction Error (Schultz)
The brain modeled X. The line delivered Y. The gap is the dopamine.

**Construction patterns:**
- Invert a domain consensus: "I fired my best engineer on a Tuesday. Her code was clean."
- Specific number against expectation: "We grew 0% in Q4. On purpose."
- Status inversion: "We turned down $2M from a16z last March."

### Open Loop (Zeigarnik)
Open the wound. Refuse the bandage in the first sentence.

**Construction patterns:**
- Named gap, undefined: "The thing nobody warned me about Series A is the part that broke my marriage."
- Temporal cliff: "It took me 11 years to figure out what one Stripe email said in 4 lines."
- Personal incomplete: "I almost shut Atlas down on a Tuesday in March. Then a contractor sent me one screenshot."

### Pattern Interrupt (Erickson)
Break the platform's expected grammar in frame one.

**Construction patterns by medium:**
- X opener with no `you`: "Most threads start with the word 'you.' This one doesn't."
- LinkedIn opener with admission: "I made a $217K mistake in Q2."
- TikTok with silence: text-on-frame + 4 seconds of silence before voice enters.
- Email subject in lowercase fragment: `i was wrong about you`

### Dissonance (Music Theory)
Two truths that should not coexist, held in one sentence.

**Construction patterns:**
- Sacred + profane: "I learned to run a board meeting from a strip-club manager in Tampa."
- Privilege + struggle: "I closed a $4M round on Friday and couldn't afford my therapist on Monday."
- Joy + grief: "I shipped my best work the week of my mother's funeral. The two facts are connected."

### Social Frame (NLP)
Say the unsayable thing the room is too polite to admit.

**Construction patterns:**
- Saying the unsayable: "I never wanted to be a founder. The role landed on me when my co-founder went to rehab."
- Admitting the publicly forbidden: "I haven't read half the books I quote. Including the ones in my pinned tweet."
- Status confession: "I make less than two of my employees. I structured it that way."

## 2. BEFORE/AFTER — SLOP vs DEPLOYMENT-READY

The single highest-leverage section in this document. Read every pair before generating.

Slop is recognizable. It uses Campbell-stage labels as decoration. It speaks to "anyone who's ever felt." It rounds dates and numbers off. It applies Vonnegut shapes the way a beginner applies eyeliner — in the wrong place, at the wrong angle. The good versions below pass the Specificity Gate (named entities, exact numbers, dated moments) and the Slop Test (would a thousand other founders also write this exact sentence — yes or no).

### PAIR 1 — HOOK

**SLOP:**
> "I almost gave up on my dream. Then everything changed."

What's wrong: fortune-cookie phrase ("almost gave up"), no specifics (which dream, when, what changed), zero threshold violations, universal-welcome voice. A thousand founders wrote this sentence today.

**DEPLOYS:**
> "I almost killed Atlas in March. The thing that saved it was a 4-line email from a contractor we'd already let go."

Why it lands: named entity (Atlas), dated moment (March), specific artifact (4-line email), open loop (which contractor, what email), prediction error (the saver is the person you fired), social frame violation (admitting near-death publicly).

### PAIR 2 — HOOK

**SLOP:**
> "For anyone who has ever felt like an outsider in their industry, this is for you."

What's wrong: universal-welcome voice (the cardinal sin — slay-script is filtration, not invitation), no anchor to the user's actual story, no threshold violation, reads like a Mailchimp template subject line.

**DEPLOYS:**
> "I sat in 11 years of agency pitches before I realized I was the only Black queer woman in every room. The 12th year, I stopped showing up."

Why it lands: exact tenure (11 years), specific identity stack (Black queer woman), specific count (every room), decisive action with date implied (the 12th year), social-frame violation, dissonance (the visible person is the invisible person).

### PAIR 3 — MINI-MYTHOLOGY

**SLOP:**
> "I started my career full of hope. The journey began when I took a leap of faith. There were ups and downs along the way. But I never gave up. Today, I help others find their path."

What's wrong: every sentence is a fortune cookie ("full of hope," "leap of faith," "ups and downs," "never gave up," "find their path"). Vague Campbell stage labels with no actual stage execution — no Ordeal, no Return with Elixir, just gauze. Generic Vonnegut shape (claims to be New Testament but compresses to nothing). 5 sentences, 0 specifics. This is mythology cosplay.

**DEPLOYS — The Insider Defector, Old Testament shape:**
> "I made other people's brands famous for 12 years at three of the top-five agencies. I shipped 47 launches. Three of them I'm proud of. The other 44 were good and forgettable, which I now believe is worse than bad. I'm building Maya & Co because the agency model is structurally incapable of making the third kind of work, and I have the receipts."

Why it lands: exact tenure (12 years), named tier (top-five agencies), exact counts (47 launches, 3 proud, 44 forgettable), thesis statement that risks something ("worse than bad"), named brand (Maya & Co), defector posture earned through receipts. Stages executed: Refusal of the Call (the 44), Ordeal (the realization), Return with Elixir (Maya & Co with receipts). 5 sentences.

### PAIR 4 — MINI-MYTHOLOGY

**SLOP — Cinderella misapplied:**
> "I came from nothing. I worked hard. I built a company. I lost it all. Now I'm back, stronger than ever."

What's wrong: claims Cinderella shape (deep low → gains → reversal → transcendent end) but skips every required beat — no depth in the low, no distinct gains (just "worked hard"), no specific reversal, no transcendent end (just "stronger than ever," which is a participation trophy phrase). This is Cinderella in name only. Also: the Ordeal is skipped, which is Campbell's most-skipped stage.

**DEPLOYS — Cinderella, properly executed:**
> "I was 19, sleeping in my car in a Ralph's parking lot in Long Beach, ghostwriting essays for $12 a piece. By 27, I had a 14-person agency and a feature in Fast Company. At 29, my biggest client — 60% of revenue — pulled out over one Slack message I'd sent at 2am. I shut the agency down on a Friday and spent six months not telling my parents. The version of me writing this owns the IP to the system that client tried to keep, and licenses it to four of their competitors."

Why it lands: depth of low is named (Ralph's parking lot, Long Beach, $12), two distinct gains (14-person agency, Fast Company feature), specific catastrophic reversal (60% client over one 2am Slack), Ordeal is on-page (six months not telling parents), transcendent end is concrete and ironic (licensing to the competitors). The Cinderella shape is earned, not labeled.

### PAIR 5 — ORIGIN VARIANT

**SLOP — Forced Vonnegut shape:**
> "I had it all, then I lost it, then I got it back, but in a totally different way."

What's wrong: this is Boy-Meets-Girl in label only — no normalcy named, no specific find, no specific loss, no transformed return. The shape is being applied as decoration to a story that has no actual emotional curve. Worse, "totally different way" is the kind of phrase that signals the writer doesn't know what the difference is. If the shape doesn't match the user's actual emotional truth, switch shapes — don't fake it.

**DEPLOYS — Boy-Meets-Girl, Variant B for a brand origin:**
> "I'd been writing brand manifestos for SaaS companies for six years and was numb to the work. In 2022, I took a freelance gig writing for a fourth-generation soap maker in Aleppo. The contract ended after eight weeks and I went back to SaaS, expecting to feel nothing. Three months in, I quit my agency and rebuilt my entire practice around the soap-maker's brief: heritage businesses with one thing they refuse to compromise. Two of them are now retainer clients."

Why it lands: normalcy named (six years SaaS, numb), specific find (Aleppo soap maker, 2022, eight weeks), specific loss (returning to SaaS, expecting nothing), transformed return (rebuilt practice around the brief, two retainers). The shape is doing structural work, not decorative work.

## 3. The 6 Voice Postures

Postures, not formulas. The writer adopts the stance — body language, eye contact, what they refuse to apologize for. The template below each is scaffolding, not a fill-in-the-blank.

### Posture 1: The Reluctant Prophet
**Stance:** "I didn't choose this work. The work chose me at the wrong moment and I never got rid of it."
The reluctance is the credential. Avoid: martyr energy. The reluctant prophet doesn't complain — they testify.

**Scaffolding:**
> "I [didn't ask for / didn't plan to] [the work]. I built [the named thing] because [the moment that forced it, dated]. I almost [the path not taken, named]. The version of me from [specific year] would have [counterfactual]. The version writing this [present truth, with receipt]."

**Best for:** build-authority, reposition.

### Posture 2: The Insider Defector
**Stance:** "I built the thing I'm now warning you about. I have receipts. I'm not asking you to trust me — I'm telling you what I shipped."
Defector status is irreplicable, but only if the user was actually inside. If they weren't, this posture is fraud (see FRAMEWORK ABUSE PATTERNS).

**Scaffolding:**
> "I spent [N years] [the thing the audience needs to escape] at [named context]. I [shipped / sold / built] [specific output, counted]. Then [the realization, dated]. I'm building [new thing] because [the original sin] is real and I watched it from [specific seat in the room]."

**Best for:** reposition, category-enter, viral.

### Posture 3: The Pattern Spotter
**Stance:** "I noticed something everyone else is missing, and I can prove I noticed it first."
The authority is in the receipts of early sight. Vague pattern claims are slop. Three specific instances minimum.

**Scaffolding:**
> "I noticed it first in [specific small detail, dated]. Then [second instance, dated]. Then [third instance, scaled]. Most [domain experts] miss this because [structural reason — incentives, training, vantage]. The pattern is [the insight in one sentence]. The implication is [the actionable conclusion]."

**Best for:** build-authority, viral, idea/thesis.

### Posture 4: The Mentor's Mentor
**Stance:** "I learned this from someone who paid for the lesson, and I refuse to let you pay the same way."
Borrowed authority through a named source. The gift is preventing pain.

**Scaffolding:**
> "[Specific person, with credential and context] taught me [the thing] in [year/place]. They learned it from [the disaster they survived, named]. I've watched [N other people] learn the same lesson the same way. I refuse to let you join them."

**Best for:** speaking-gig, build-authority.

### Posture 5: The Apostate
**Stance:** "I used to preach the consensus. I broke with it publicly. I paid for the break and I'm not going back."
Public conversion is high-engagement. The cost must be specific or it reads as repositioning theater.

**Scaffolding:**
> "I used to [believe / preach / sell] [the consensus position]. I [taught / wrote / built] around it for [N years]. Then [the breaking moment, dated]. Now I [the new position]. The shift cost me [specific thing — N followers, $X clients, named relationships]. I'm not going back."

**Best for:** reposition, viral, idea/thesis.

### Posture 6: The Witness
**Stance:** "I was in the room. I watched the change happen. I'm telling you what the press release left out."
Witness status is unfakeable. The seat in the room is the credential.

**Scaffolding:**
> "I was [in the room / at the table / in the work] when [the inflection point, dated]. I watched [the specific change]. Most people heard about it [later, sanitized]. I'm telling you what actually happened, including the part [reason it was hidden]."

**Best for:** speaking-gig, build-authority, brand.

## 4. The 8 Vonnegut Shape Generators

Pick the shape that matches the user's emotional truth. Forcing a shape that doesn't match the story produces slop (see FRAMEWORK ABUSE PATTERNS).

### Man-in-Hole
**Curve:** bearable → fall → recovery → higher than before.
**Best for:** founder origins with a recoverable disaster.
**Length:** 4–5 sentences.
**Required beats:** the bearable status quo (named), the specific fall (dated), the named recovery mechanism (not "I worked hard"), the post-recovery position with receipt.

### Boy-Meets-Girl
**Curve:** normal → finds something valuable → loses it → gets it back transformed.
**Best for:** product discovery, "I didn't know I needed this," brand origin via unexpected encounter.
**Length:** 4–6 sentences.
**Required beats:** the normalcy (named), the find (specific), the loss (specific), the transformed return (concrete, not metaphorical).

### Cinderella
**Curve:** deep misfortune → series of gains → catastrophic reversal → transcendent end.
**Best for:** underdog brand origins, "we had nothing and now we're everything."
**Length:** 5–6 sentences.
**Required beats:** the depth of low (placed, dated, specific), ≥2 distinct gains (not one gain twice), the reversal (named cause), the transcendence (concrete, not "stronger than ever").

### Old Testament (Riches to Rags)
**Curve:** high status → fall → wisdom (no return to status).
**Best for:** Insider Defector posture; warning narratives.
**Length:** 4–6 sentences.
**Required beats:** high status named with specifics (firm, title, comp), the fall, the lesson that cannot be re-bought.

### New Testament (Rags to Riches)
**Curve:** low status → struggle → transcendent status.
**Best for:** personal transformation, bootstrapped origin.
**Length:** 4–5 sentences.
**Required beats:** the genuinely-low low (named, not "I came from nothing"), the specific struggle, the transcendent (not just "successful") high.

### Hamlet (The Staircase)
**Curve:** oscillation between fortune and misfortune; no clean resolution.
**Best for:** multi-chapter brand stories, content series, ongoing mythos.
**Length:** 5–6 sentences.
**Required beats:** ≥3 reversals, each one genuinely changing the reader's model of the story.

### Kafka (Unreversed Fall)
**Curve:** normal → catastrophe → no recovery; the world is permanently altered.
**Best for:** urgency narratives, "the world is changing and you will be left behind."
**Length:** 4–5 sentences.
**Required beats:** the normalcy (named), the catastrophe (dated), the explicit absence of recovery (no "but I came back").

### Flatline (Which Way Is Up)
**Curve:** no clear up or down; the ambiguity is the point.
**Best for:** thought leadership, philosophical positioning, brand manifestos.
**Length:** 5–6 sentences.
**Required beats:** the ambiguity is intentional and productive, not vague. If the reader can't tell whether you mean it as a virtue or a wound, you've succeeded — but they have to feel the choice.

## 5. Campbell Stage Compression Manual

A 4-6 sentence mythology cannot run all 12 stages. The craft is selecting which 3–5 stages to compress for the desired effect.

### High-impact stage subsets

**Subset A — The Defector Subset (Old Testament):**
Refusal of the Call → Approach to Inmost Cave → Ordeal → Return with Elixir.
For Insider Defector and Apostate. The "I built it / I broke it / I'm back with receipts" arc.

**Subset B — The Witness Subset (Hamlet or Kafka):**
Ordinary World → Crossing the Threshold → Ordeal → Reward.
For Witness and Pattern Spotter. The "I was there / I saw / I'm telling you" arc.

**Subset C — The Reluctant Subset (Man-in-Hole or New Testament):**
Refusal of the Call → Meeting the Mentor → Ordeal → Resurrection.
For Reluctant Prophet and Mentor's Mentor. The "I didn't want this / I learned anyway / I'm here now" arc.

**Subset D — The Origin Subset (Cinderella or Old Testament):**
Ordinary World → Call to Adventure → Crossing the Threshold → Tests, Allies, Enemies → Return with Elixir.
For full origin stories where the variant has 5–6 sentences to breathe.

### The two non-skippable stages

In any 4-6 sentence mythology, these cannot be cut:

1. **The Ordeal** — the moment of genuine vulnerability. Without it, the story is a brochure.
2. **The Return / Resurrection / Reward** — the arrival back, with something the reader can see. Without it, the story is a setup with no payoff.

If a draft skips either, regenerate. The Ordeal is Campbell's most-skipped stage in lazy mythology — guard it.

## 6. The Deploy-Map Decision Tree

Every output answers: where does this go, why, in what sequence.

### Hooks
- Highest threshold-violation density → X opening tweet.
- Strongest open-loop → email subject line.
- Strongest social-frame violation → LinkedIn opener.
- Strongest pattern-interrupt → TikTok first frame.
- Strongest dissonance → podcast guest opener.

### Mini-Mythologies
- Reluctant Prophet → about-page intro.
- Insider Defector → founder-pitch email opener.
- Pattern Spotter → pinned tweet.
- Mentor's Mentor → speaking-pitch deck slide 1.
- Apostate → newsletter intro post (re-positioning announcement).
- Witness → podcast intro.

### Origin Variants
- Variant A (most universal shape) → about page.
- Variant B (most polarizing shape) → editorial / op-ed pitch.
- Variant C (most niche shape) → deep-fan content (community channel, DM template).

## 7. FRAMEWORK ABUSE PATTERNS

The actual ways these playbooks get butchered in the wild. If you catch yourself doing any of these, regenerate — don't edit.

- **Cinderella cosplay.** Slapping the Cinderella label on a story that isn't actually Cinderella (no depth in the low, no distinct gains, no catastrophic reversal). **Fix:** verify all four required beats are on-page; if two are missing, switch shapes.

- **Skipping the Ordeal.** Campbell's most-skipped stage in lazy mythology. The draft jumps from struggle to triumph with no on-page vulnerability. **Fix:** the Ordeal must be a sentence the user would hesitate to publish. If you wouldn't hesitate, it isn't the Ordeal.

- **Forced Vonnegut shapes.** Applying Boy-Meets-Girl to a story that's actually Kafka because Kafka feels too dark. The shape doesn't match the user's emotional truth. **Fix:** generate the shape the story actually has, then warn the user if it's commercially uncomfortable. Don't lie with the curve.

- **Defector fraud.** Using Insider Defector posture when the user wasn't actually inside the thing they're warning about. **Fix:** require receipts — named firm, named tenure, named output. If the receipts don't exist, switch postures (Pattern Spotter and Apostate work without inside seats).

- **Three-sentence mythology.** Calling 3 sentences a mini-mythology when the spec requires 4–6. Compression past the floor strips out the Ordeal or the Return. **Fix:** 4-sentence floor, 6-sentence ceiling, no exceptions.

- **Vague Campbell labels as decoration.** Tagging a draft with "Campbell stages: Refusal, Ordeal, Return" when no actual stage is executed in the prose. **Fix:** point to the sentence that executes each named stage. If you can't point, the stage isn't there.

- **Universal-welcome voice.** "For anyone who has ever felt..." — slay-script is filtration, not invitation. **Fix:** the reader is named or implied to within a tight cohort. The wrong reader should feel the door close.

- **Fortune-cookie phrases.** "I almost gave up," "the journey began," "leap of faith," "trust the process," "everything changed." These are autopilot phrases the threshold protocols exist to violate. **Fix:** banned in drafts. Replace each with a dated, specific, named alternative or cut the sentence entirely.

- **Round numbers and undated moments.** "A few years ago," "a lot of money," "many clients." Specificity is the gate. **Fix:** real numbers (47, not "many"), real dates (March 2022, not "a few years ago"), real names (Atlas, not "the company").
