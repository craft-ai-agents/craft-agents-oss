# SERVIN' Rubric — Pillar Definitions & Scoring Guide

## Scoring Scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 90-100 | A | Exceptional. Future-facing audacity. Shifts culture. Reference-worthy. |
| 80-89 | B | Solid, professional work. Minor principle violations. Safe. |
| 70-79 | C | Functional but uninspired. Several principles violated. |
| 60-69 | D | Problematic. Core principles systematically broken. |
| 50-59 | F | Failing. Fundamental principles violated. UX degraded. |
| 0-49 | F- | Actively harmful. WCAG failures, cognitive overload. Start over. |

**Critical Rule — THE DARE Mandatory Gate:**
No artifact scores above **85** without a viable **DARE** — one audacious, envelope-pushing recommendation that future-casts the artifact by at least +5 years on the Zeitgeist timeline and achieves a Polarization Score of ≥60%. If the artifact is flawlessly executed but entirely predictable (no dare, no future-casting, no polarizing audacity), **cap the Overall Score at 85** regardless of pillar perfection.

**Vision Layer Cap:**
If an artifact scores perfectly on all six pillars but fails the 2050 Test (locked to 2D rectangles, static wallpaper, no generative/living/spatial ambition), the Vision Layer score caps at **75**. Competence without audacity is a ceiling, not a floor.

## Pillar 1: Silhouette (Weight: 1.0x)

**Governing Principles:** Gestalt Figure/Ground, Law of Prägnanz (Good Form), Closure, Common Region.
**Vision Layer Lens:** McQueen Theatre — Narrative Architecture, Act I/II/III Structure, Emotional Violence.

**Question:** Does the overall shape read instantly? Does the squint test pass? Does the silhouette tell a story that unsettles before it seduces?

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** Squint test passes at 20% blur. Dominant form legible even obscured. **AND** the silhouette has narrative architecture — an Act I (setup that creates expectation), Act II (twist that violates it), Act III (resolution that recontextualizes). The form is not just legible; it is *unforgettable*. McQueen-level emotional violence: the silhouette unsettles before it seduces. Single Gestalt closure achieved — mind perceives complete form without detail. Uses one dominant strategy: centered monument, asymmetric split (60/40 or 70/30), full-bleed hero, or radial concentration. Figure/Ground separation is absolute. **This is a silhouette that would be studied in 2050.**
- **80-89 (B):** Structure legible at normal view. Boundaries clear. Basic Common Region present. No strong dominant form, but no confusion. No narrative architecture — linear, predictable, safe.
- **70-79 (C):** Dominant form is weak. Multiple layout strategies compete. Law of Prägnanz violated — simplest organization not perceived because complexity is visible without purpose.
- **60-69 (D):** Structure muddled. Sidebars fight center content. Elements float without anchoring. Figure/Ground ambiguous — layers collapse. Closure broken.
- **50-59 (F):** No clear outer shape. Rectangle of rectangles. Common Region absent.
- **<50 (F-):** Chaos without form. Visual search triggered immediately.

### Vision Layer Audit (McQueen Theatre Test)
- **Narrative Shock:** Does the silhouette tell a story? Is there an unexpected form, an asymmetry, a void, or a tension that makes the viewer pause before they understand?
- **Act I/II/III:** Act I = the shape that creates expectation. Act II = the element that breaks it (the void, the intrusion, the off-center mass). Act III = the resolution (the eye finds the focal point and the silhouette resolves into meaning). If all three acts are not present, the silhouette is a flat shape, not a narrative.
- **Emotional Violence:** Does the silhouette contain tension, discomfort, or risk? Safe silhouettes (perfectly centered, perfectly balanced, perfectly predictable) are forgettable. Dangerous silhouettes (deliberately off-balance, intentionally incomplete, provocatively asymmetric) are iconic.
- **Audit action:** If the silhouette can be described without emotion — "it's a centered rectangle with a sidebar" — it fails the McQueen Theatre Test. If it requires a story — "the hero bleeds off the left edge while the CTA hovers in negative space like a threat" — it passes.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **Squint Test:** Close eyes to slits. Can you identify dominant shape and primary focal point?
- **Blur Test (Mental):** At 20px Gaussian blur, does structural form still communicate?
- **Figure/Ground Test:** Is there clear separation between active foreground and supporting background?
- **Narrative Test:** Can you describe the silhouette in three acts? If not, it is a shape, not a story.

### Common Fixes
- Add full-bleed hero with an asymmetric void (Act I: expectation of fullness; Act II: the void breaks it; Act III: the eye finds the CTA in the void).
- Create an asymmetric split where the smaller side carries the emotional weight (McQueen's "Highland Rape" — beauty through disproportion).
- Use a framing device that is deliberately broken or incomplete (a border that stops halfway, a background block that bleeds off-edge).
- Make one element intentionally oversized to the point of discomfort — then anchor it with a tiny, precise counter-element.
- Remove competing layout strategies. One dominant structure per viewport. But that structure must *risk something*.

## Pillar 2: Hierarchy (Weight: 1.0x)

**Governing Principles:** Hick's Law, Visual Weight Distribution, F/Z/Layer-Cake Scanning Patterns, Gestalt Similarity.
**Vision Layer Lens:** Kanye Pivot — Genre-Breaking, Maximalist-Minimalist Swings, Disruptive Taste.

**Question:** Is there a clear 1-2-3? Does the eye know where to go? Does the hierarchy genre-break — does it pivot violently between restraint and excess, between expected and impossible?

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** One dominant element commands attention with 2× visual weight of secondary. Eye movement choreographed. **AND** the hierarchy contains a Kanye-level pivot: a maximalist-minimalist swing, a genre-breaking fusion, or a deliberate violation of the expected scanning path that redefines the viewer's relationship with the content. The hierarchy is not just clear — it is *shocking in its clarity*. Hick's Law respected at the foundation (one primary per zone) but the zone itself may be redefined (a single zone that contains a typographic monument *and* a whispered subhead, creating tension through scale disparity). Gestalt Similarity groups related info, separates unrelated — but the groupings themselves may fuse unexpected categories (poetry + dashboard, fashion + data viz).
- **80-89 (B):** Primary element clear. Secondary elements compete mildly. Scanning pattern mostly aligned. No genre-breaking. No maximalist-minimalist swing. Predictable hierarchy.
- **70-79 (C):** Two things claim "first." Hick's Law violated — competing visual weights increase decision time.
- **60-69 (D):** Three or more claim "first." Layout requires active visual search. Key actions sit outside expected paths.
- **50-59 (F):** Everything same size/weight/color. Or everything different and competing. No weight distribution.
- **<50 (F-):** Active disorientation. Cannot determine artifact's purpose within 5 seconds.

### Vision Layer Audit (Kanye Pivot Test)
- **Genre-Breaking:** Does the hierarchy fuse categories that "shouldn't" fuse? A headline that behaves like a photograph. A data visualization that behaves like a poem. A navigation that behaves like a sculpture. If the hierarchy is pure to one genre (standard SaaS, standard editorial, standard e-commerce), it is competent but not visionary.
- **Maximalist-Minimalist Swing:** Is there a moment of absolute restraint (one word, one color, one pixel) adjacent to a moment of absolute excess (oversized type, saturated color, dense texture)? The swing itself is the taste. The confidence to be both is the vision. If the hierarchy is consistently moderate — never too much, never too little — it is wallpaper.
- **The Pivot Test:** Can you identify one element that, if removed, would make the hierarchy feel like every other hierarchy in its category? That element is the pivot. If there is no pivot, the taste is generic.
- **Audit action:** Describe the hierarchy in terms of emotional dynamics, not just visual weight. "The headline screams, the subhead whispers, the CTA waits in silence." If the description is flat — "H1 is largest, H2 is medium, CTA is prominent" — the hierarchy fails the Kanye Pivot Test.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **Hick's Law Audit:** Count competing visual weights per zone. >1 dominant per zone = violation.
- **Scanning Path Test:** Trace expected F/Z/Layer-Cake path. Are critical actions on this path? **BONUS:** Is the path itself redefined — does the hierarchy force a new scanning behavior?
- **Visual Weight Calculation:** Size (×2), saturation (×1.5), contrast (×2), position (×1.5). Highest score should be primary action. **BONUS:** Is the weight distribution itself the story? (A tiny element with maximum contrast that hijacks attention from a massive but low-contrast element.)
- **Genre Fusion Test:** List the genres present in the hierarchy. If only one genre is detectable, the hierarchy is pure and predictable. If two or more are fused with purpose, the hierarchy is visionary.

### Common Fixes
- H1 minimum 2× subhead size — **but** make the H1 so large it risks illegibility, then anchor it with a precisely measured subhead.
- Primary CTA uses unique color — **but** make the CTA color so unexpected (not the industry default) that it polarizes. The color itself is the pivot.
- Reduce competing elements 30% in size or 50% in opacity — **then** add one unexpected element at 150% scale that breaks the pattern. The reduction creates the space; the excess creates the memory.
- Align layout with correct scanning pattern — **then** place one critical action *outside* the pattern, forcing the viewer to discover it. The discovery is the intrigue.
- Group related items via Proximity — **then** place one unrelated item inside the group, creating productive tension. The tension is the story.

## Pillar 3: Contrast (Weight: 1.0x)

**Governing Principles:** WCAG 2.1 Contrast Ratios, Value/Lightness Separation, Chromatic Distinction, Tesler's Law, Grayscale Hierarchy Test.
**Vision Layer Lens:** Emotional Violence — Contrast as Provocation, Polarizing Palette, Beauty Through Darkness.

**Question:** Do the right things separate? Is contrast intentional, controlled, and accessible? Does the contrast polarize — does it create emotional response, not just visual clarity?

### Specific Measurements
| Element | WCAG AA Minimum | WCAG AAA Minimum |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| Normal body text | 4.5:1 | 7:1 |
| Large text (18pt+ or 14pt+ bold) | 3:1 | 4.5:1 |
| UI components (borders, icons, focus) | 3:1 | — |

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** Contrast intentional, limited to strategic pairs. WCAG AA met/exceeded for all text. **AND** the contrast choices are polarizing — they create a binary emotional response (love/hate, comfort/discomfort, desire/repulsion). The palette is not just functional; it is *provocative*. McQueen-level emotional violence: the contrast unsettles before it seduces. The grayscale hierarchy test passes — hierarchy preserved without hue. **AND** the contrast includes one deliberate violation: a color pairing that "shouldn't" work, a value inversion that challenges expectation, or a chromatic tension that feels dangerous. The dangerous pairing is the pivot.
- **80-89 (B):** Basic contrast exists and mostly strategic. Text passes WCAG AA. No polarizing choices. No dangerous pairings. Safe, professional, forgettable.
- **70-79 (C):** Contrast uneven. Some elements vanish. Others scream unexpectedly. Grayscale test partially fails.
- **60-69 (D):** Contrast chaotic. Low contrast everywhere or high contrast chaos. WCAG AA failures present.
- **50-59 (F):** Systematic contrast failure. Text unreadable. Interactive elements indistinguishable.
- **<50 (F-):** Actively inaccessible. Color is sole carrier of meaning.

### Vision Layer Audit (Emotional Violence Test)
- **Polarizing Palette:** Does the color palette create a binary response? A palette that pleases everyone pleases no one. A palette that makes 30% of viewers actively uncomfortable and 70% obsessed is a polarizing palette. The 30% will share, screenshot, and debate. The 70% will defend it.
- **Beauty Through Darkness:** Is there a moment of contrast that feels dangerous, uncomfortable, or transgressive? (McQueen's skull motifs — horrifying and beautiful simultaneously.) If all contrast choices feel "safe" and "professional," the contrast is wallpaper.
- **The Dangerous Pairing:** Identify one color/value/saturation pairing that breaks conventional wisdom. If there is no dangerous pairing, the contrast is competent but not visionary.
- **Audit action:** Show the palette to 10 people. If fewer than 3 have a strong negative reaction, the palette is not polarizing enough. If fewer than 5 have a strong positive reaction, the palette is not seductive enough.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **Grayscale Filter Test:** Convert mentally to grayscale. Does hierarchy hold? If collapses, design relies on hue alone.
- **WCAG Spot Check:** Identify 3 highest-contrast and 3 lowest-contrast text/background pairs. Report ratios.
- **Contrast Mechanism Count:** Count mechanisms used (color, shadow, size, weight, border, background). >3 without clear system = chaos.
- **Emotional Response Test:** Does the contrast evoke a physical sensation? (Warmth, coldness, weight, depth, threat, invitation.) If the contrast is purely cognitive ("I can read this"), it is functional. If it is visceral ("I feel something"), it is visionary.

### Common Fixes
- Audit grayscale — if elements merge, increase size/weight/value, not just hue.
- Pick ONE primary mechanism (value/lightness) and ONE accent (color). **Then** add one dangerous pairing: a low-value warm against a high-value cool, a saturated accent against a desaturated ground, a color that "doesn't belong" in the industry palette.
- Ensure body text ≥4.5:1. Accessibility is non-negotiable. But the *accent* contrast can violate expectations. The accessible foundation supports the dangerous expression.
- For data viz: use pattern + color, not color alone. **Then** make one data point so visually dominant it feels like a threat. The threat is the story.

## Pillar 4: Rhythm (Weight: 1.0x)

**Governing Principles:** 8pt/4pt Grid, Vertical Rhythm (Baseline Grid), Modular Type Scale, Gestalt Continuity, White Space Psychology (Macro/Micro).
**Vision Layer Lens:** Generative & Living Systems — The Rhythm Breathes, Adapts, Responds. Future-Cast Rhythm.

**Question:** Is there visual breathing room and a consistent, predictable pulse? **OR** is the rhythm generative — does it adapt, respond, and breathe like a living system?

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** Spacing system evident, ≥90% adherence. 8pt grid governs all dimensions. Modular type scale defines typography. Vertical rhythm consistent. **AND** the rhythm is not just consistent — it is *alive*. The spacing creates narrative pacing: macro white space that accelerates and decelerates like a film edit, micro white space that breathes with the content. The rhythm has a heartbeat, not just a metronome. **OR** the rhythm is future-cast: it anticipates spatial computing (depth layers, z-axis rhythm), ambient interfaces (rhythm that responds to context), or generative layouts (rhythm that adapts to content). The rhythm is not a grid; it is a *system*.
- **80-89 (B):** Spacing mostly consistent (70-89% on-grid). Few outliers break pulse. Type sizes show loose progression. No generative ambition. No future-casting. Predictable rhythm.
- **70-79 (C):** Spacing partially systematic. Margins vary without clear scale. Type sizes arbitrary. Macro white space uneven.
- **60-69 (D):** Spacing arbitrary. No grid logic. Sections cramped or loose unpredictably.
- **50-59 (F):** No spacing logic. Elements touch or float randomly.
- **<50 (F-):** Rhythm actively hostile. Visually assaultive.

### Vision Layer Audit (Generative Rhythm Test)
- **The Heartbeat Test:** Does the rhythm have variation — acceleration, deceleration, pause, crescendo? Or is it a metronome (8, 16, 24, 32, repeat)? A metronome is functional. A heartbeat is emotional. A heartbeat makes the viewer feel the passage of time.
- **Future-Cast Rhythm:** Does the rhythm anticipate 2050 interfaces? Spatial depth (z-axis spacing that creates layers), ambient adaptation (spacing that changes based on context), generative variation (spacing that responds to content density). If the rhythm is locked to a 2D flat grid, it is 2020. If the rhythm creates depth, motion, or adaptation, it is 2050.
- **Macro Pacing as Narrative:** The space between sections should tell a story. Tight spacing = tension. Loose spacing = release. Alternating tight/loose = narrative rhythm. If every section has the same spacing, there is no story.
- **Audit action:** Map the spacing values as a sequence. Does the sequence have a narrative arc? (e.g., 16→48→16→96→16 — tight, release, tight, expansive, tight = a story of tension and release.) If the sequence is flat, the rhythm is a spreadsheet, not a symphony.

### Specific Measurements
- **8pt Grid Adherence:** Count on-grid (multiples of 8) vs off-grid. ≥90% = excellent. 70-89% = good. 50-69% = problematic. <50% = broken.
- **Modular Type Scale Detection:** Check if sizes follow consistent ratio from base. Arbitrary = no system.
- **Line Length:** Body text should be 45-75 characters per line (max 90).
- **Line Height:** Body text 1.4-1.6× font size. Display 1.2-1.3×.
- **Rhythm Narrative Arc:** Map spacing sequence. Does it have variation ≥2× between minimum and maximum? If not, the rhythm is flat.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **The Heartbeat Test:** Read the spacing sequence aloud as numbers. Does it sound like a rhythm or a list? (16, 24, 32, 40 = list. 16, 64, 16, 128 = rhythm.)
- **Generative Test:** Could this rhythm adapt to a different content volume without breaking? If the rhythm is content-dependent (requires exact word counts), it is fragile. If the rhythm is content-agnostic (a system), it is generative.
- **Depth Test:** Does the rhythm create depth? Are there spacing values that imply layers, distance, or z-axis? If all spacing is horizontal/vertical, the rhythm is flat.

### Common Fixes
- Define spacing scale 8,16,24,32,40,48,56,64,72,80,96. **Then** add one "shock" spacing (128, 192, or 256) used sparingly — once per page. The shock creates the narrative peak.
- Implement modular type scale (1.25 ratio, 16px base). **Then** add one "monument" size (96px, 128px, or 192px) that breaks the scale. The monument is the pivot.
- Set body line-height 1.5×, cap line length at 75 characters. **Then** create one paragraph with line-height 2.0× and line length 30 characters. The disruption creates intimacy.
- Align all left edges to single vertical axis. **Then** break the alignment once — one element that floats freely, creating productive tension. The tension is the story.
- Double space between major sections if cramped. **Then** halve the space between two unrelated sections, forcing the viewer to bridge the gap mentally. The bridge is the engagement.

## Pillar 5: Restraint (Weight: 1.0x)

**Governing Principles:** Miller's Law (7±2), Tesler's Law, Von Restorff Isolation Discipline.
**Vision Layer Lens:** Maximalist-Minimalist Discipline — The Confidence to Be Excessive, The Discipline to Be Bare.

**Question:** What was removed? Does every element earn its place? **AND** does the restraint know when to break — does the system have the confidence to be utterly excessive at the precise moment it matters?

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** Nothing removable without functional/compositional loss. Miller's Law respected. Von Restorff disciplined: ONE distinctive element per view. **AND** the restraint contains a maximalist-minimalist swing: a section of absolute austerity (one word, one color, one element) adjacent to a section of absolute excess (dense, layered, maximal). The swing is not chaos — it is *controlled demolition*. The excess is so excessive it makes the restraint feel luxurious. The restraint is so restrained it makes the excess feel explosive. Tesler's Law applied — complexity absorbed by system, not dumped on user. The complexity is in the *architecture of the swing*, not in the surface clutter.
- **80-89 (B):** Mostly focused. Few "just in case" elements. No maximalist-minimalist swing. Consistent moderation throughout. Safe.
- **70-79 (C):** Feature bloat visible. Multiple CTAs per section. Tesler's Law violated.
- **60-69 (D):** Miller's Law systematically broken. Navigation >10 items. Layouts >9 visual groups.
- **50-59 (F):** Everything included. Von Restorff inverted — everything is distinctive, so nothing is.
- **<50 (F-):** Cognitive overload dominant. Users abandon before comprehension.

### Vision Layer Audit (Maximalist-Minimalist Discipline Test)
- **The Swing Test:** Is there a moment of absolute minimalism (one element, one color, one word) and a moment of absolute maximalism (layered, dense, complex) within the same artifact? If the entire artifact is moderate — never too much, never too little — it is a lullaby, not a symphony.
- **The Confidence Test:** Does the maximalist section feel *earned* because the minimalist section created the space for it? Or does it feel like clutter? The earned excess is architecture. The unearned excess is mess.
- **The Discipline Test:** Does the minimalist section feel *luxurious* because the maximalist section proved the system can handle complexity? Or does it feel empty? The luxurious void is confidence. The empty void is failure.
- **Audit action:** Identify the most restrained section and the most excessive section. If the distance between them is <3× in visual density, the artifact lacks the swing. If the distance is >10×, the swing is architectural. If there is no excessive section, the artifact is afraid. If there is no restrained section, the artifact is shouting.

### Specific Measurements
- Navigation: ≤7 = excellent. 8-9 = acceptable with grouping. >9 = Miller's Law violated.
- CTAs per section: 1 primary = ideal. 2 = acceptable if secondary is subordinate. >2 = failure.
- Form fields per view: ≤7 = ideal. 8-10 = acceptable with grouping. >10 without sectioning = violation.
- Visual groups per view: ≤7 = ideal.
- Distinctive elements per view: 1 = powerful (Von Restorff). 2 = acceptable. >2 = noise. 0 = forgettable.
- **Maximalist-Minimalist Ratio:** Identify the section with lowest visual density (elements per pixel) and highest visual density. Calculate the ratio. <3× = insufficient swing. 3-10× = good swing. >10× = architectural swing.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **The Swing Test:** Does the artifact contain both a "breath" (section you can read in one glance) and a "meal" (section that rewards prolonged attention)? If only breaths: shallow. If only meals: exhausting.
- **The Earned Excess Test:** Remove the maximalist section. Does the minimalist section now feel empty rather than luxurious? If yes, the excess was earning the restraint. If no, the excess was clutter.
- **The Earned Restraint Test:** Remove the minimalist section. Does the maximalist section now feel overwhelming rather than energetic? If yes, the restraint was earning the excess. If no, the restraint was laziness.

### Common Fixes
- Apply "one per" rule: one primary CTA per section, one value prop per above-fold, one nav level, one distinctive element per view. **Then** add one section that deliberately breaks the rule: a maximalist moment that contains 5-7 elements in a dense cluster, creating a visual event. The cluster is the exception that proves the rule.
- Chunk into groups of 5-7 (Miller's Law). **Then** create one group that is a single, massive element. The group of one is the pivot.
- Delete weakest 30% and test if anything breaks. **Then** add one element back — but make it 3× larger than before. The enlarged element is the statement.
- Absorb complexity into system via progressive disclosure. **Then** reveal the complexity in one dramatic moment: a single interaction that exposes the full system in a burst of information. The burst is the payoff.
- Use "Breathing Room Test" — if removing an element makes layout calmer without losing meaning, it was noise. **Then** add one element that, if removed, makes the layout feel *dead*. That element is the heartbeat.

## Pillar 6: The It Factor (Weight: 1.2x)

**Governing Principles:** Von Restorff Effect (Isolation), Peak-End Rule, Aesthetic-Usability Effect, Jakob's Law Risk Calibration, Brand Asset Distinctiveness, Screenshot Test.
**Vision Layer Lens:** Bowie Shift — Reinvention as Identity, Future-Casting, Zeitgeist Gap, Polarization Test.

**Question:** Is this distinctive, memorable, opinionated, and **future-facing**? Does it pass the Screenshot Test? Does it achieve a Polarization Score of ≥60%? Is it +5 years ahead of the zeitgeist?

### Scoring Thresholds

- **90+ (A):** Instantly identifiable. Passes Screenshot Test. **AND** achieves a Polarization Score of ≥60% (60-80% of viewers feel something strong — love, hate, obsession, fury). **AND** is positioned +5 to +10 years ahead on the Zeitgeist timeline (significantly ahead: misunderstood by the mainstream, "this doesn't belong here"). **AND** contains a Bowie-level reinvention: the identity is not fixed — it is a medium for constant future-casting. The artifact references an aesthetic that does not yet exist in the mainstream. The Chameleon Test is passed: the visual system could accommodate a completely different product tomorrow without breaking. One bold Von Restorff element deployed with full commitment. Peak-End Rule satisfied with narrative shock (the peak is not just memorable — it is *disruptive*). Jakob's Law respected at foundation, deliberately violated at expression layer. Recognizable in a lineup of 20 competitors. **This is not just distinctive. This is prophetic.**
- **80-89 (B):** Some personality visible. Distinctive choice present but not fully committed. Polarization Score 20-40%. Zeitgeist: on or slightly ahead (+1-3 years). Safe, trendy, not prophetic.
- **70-79 (C):** Competent but forgettable. Could be a template. Polarization Score <20%. Zeitgeist: on or behind. No Von Restorff element. No future-casting.
- **60-69 (D):** Actively generic. "Template aesthetic." Polarization Score ~0%. Zeitgeist: behind (-5 years).
- **50-59 (F):** No point of view. Could be swapped with any competitor. Peak-End Rule violated.
- **<50 (F-):** Actively off-putting. Distinctive in wrong direction.

### Vision Layer Audit (Bowie Shift Test)
- **Zeitgeist Gap:** Place the artifact on the timeline:
  - Behind (-5 years): Nostalgic, retro. Scores 40-60.
  - On (now): Current, competent. Scores 60-75.
  - Slightly ahead (+1-3 years): Trendy. Scores 75-85.
  - Significantly ahead (+5-10 years): Polarizing, misunderstood. Scores 85-95 if rigorously argued.
  - Future-cast (+10-20 years): Prototype, museum piece. Scores 95-100 if coherent, 30-50 if delusion.
- **Polarization Test:** If 100 people see this artifact, how many feel something strong?
  - <20%: Invisible. Dead.
  - 20-40%: Competent. Safe.
  - 40-60%: Interesting. Some will remember.
  - 60-80%: Polarizing. The 30% who love it will recruit. The 50% who hate it will distribute.
  - 80-100%: Avant-garde. Legendary or catastrophic.
- **The Chameleon Test:** If the brand launched a completely different product tomorrow, would the visual system accommodate the shift? Or is the identity so fragile it can only survive one context? Bowie reinvented every album. McQueen's house aesthetic survived radical collection changes. Fragile identities score <60. Chameleon identities score 90+.
- **Future-Casting:** Does the design reference a future aesthetic that does not yet exist in the mainstream? (Bowie referencing Berlin electronica in 1976. McQueen referencing extinction in 1998.) If the aesthetic is current, the It Factor is current. If the aesthetic is future, the It Factor is prophetic.

### Diagnostic Tests
- **Screenshot Test:** Would a user screenshot and share unprompted?
- **Lineup Test:** Recognizable among 20 similar artifacts in 3 seconds?
- **Peak-End Audit:** Identify peak and end. Are they memorable? Are they *disruptive*?
- **Jakob's Law Calibration:** Core interactions familiar? Is there ONE expressive layer that deliberately deviates?
- **Color Industry Test:** Is dominant accent the industry default? If yes, Von Restorff not applied.
- **Zeitgeist Timeline Test:** Ask: "If this launched in 2030, would it feel timely or dated?" If dated, it is behind. If timely, it is ahead. If laughable, it is either delusional (+20 years, no rigor) or prophetic (+20 years, coherent vision).
- **Polarization Score Test:** Describe the artifact's most distinctive choice to 5 people. Count strong reactions (positive or negative). <3 strong reactions = not polarizing enough.

### Common Fixes
- Pick ONE risk, commit 100%: single non-industry accent color, one display typeface, asymmetric split, full-bleed illustration, oversized typographic moment, micro-interaction with unexpected personality. **Then** make that risk so extreme it polarizes. The safe version of the risk is a B. The dangerous version is an A.
- Ensure peak is above fold or at natural journey conclusion. **Then** make the peak so unexpected it violates the category's conventions. The violation is the memory.
- Resolve the ending. **Then** make the ending feel like a beginning — a cliffhanger, a question, an unresolved tension. The unresolved ending is the hook.
- Apply 60-30-10 color rule. **Then** make the 10% accent so saturated, so unexpected, so "wrong" for the industry that it becomes the signature. The "wrong" color is the right color.
- **Future-cast the identity:** If the current aesthetic is 2026, what would 2031 look like for this brand? Implement one 2031 element now. The early implementation is the prophecy.

## The Vision Layer — Mandatory Integration

The Vision Layer is not an appendix. It is a **scoring multiplier** applied to every pillar. No pillar can score above 90 without passing its Vision Layer test.

### Vision Layer Scoring Matrix
| Pillar | Vision Layer Test | 90+ Requirement |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| Silhouette | McQueen Theatre Test | Narrative architecture (Act I/II/III) + emotional violence |
| Hierarchy | Kanye Pivot Test | Genre-breaking or maximalist-minimalist swing |
| Contrast | Emotional Violence Test | Polarizing palette + one dangerous pairing |
| Rhythm | Generative Rhythm Test | Heartbeat variation + future-cast ambition |
| Restraint | Maximalist-Minimalist Discipline Test | Architectural swing between austerity and excess |
| It Factor | Bowie Shift Test | +5 years zeitgeist + ≥60% polarization + chameleon identity |

### THE DARE — Mandatory Gate
Every audit must include one **DARE** in the output. The dare:
- References a future technology, cultural shift, or aesthetic not yet mainstream
- Risks being misunderstood by ≥50% of the audience
- Is implementable within existing constraints (or clearly flags if architectural)
- Achieves a Polarization Score of ≥60%
- Closes a Zeitgeist Gap of ≥+5 years

**Without THE DARE, the Overall Score is capped at 85. No exceptions.**

## Overall Score Calculation

### Step 1: Calculate Raw Pillar Score
Sum of (Pillar Score × Weight) / Total Weights.

Weights: Silhouette 1.0, Hierarchy 1.0, Contrast 1.0, Rhythm 1.0, Restraint 1.0, It Factor 1.2.

Maximum: 100 per pillar. It Factor weighted 1.2x.

### Step 2: Apply Vision Layer Cap
- No Vision Layer evidence in any pillar? Cap at **75**.
- Vision Layer present in 1-2 pillars? Cap at **85**.
- Vision Layer present in 3-4 pillars? Cap at **95**.
- Vision Layer present in 5-6 pillars? Cap at **100**.

### Step 3: Apply THE DARE Gate
- No DARE present? Cap at **85** regardless of Vision Layer.
- DARE present but weak (Polarization <60%, Zeitgeist <+5 years)? Cap at **90**.
- DARE present and strong? No additional cap. Score from Step 2 stands.

### Final Score
The lowest cap from Step 2 or Step 3 is applied to the Raw Pillar Score.

## Artifact-Type Adjustments

Different artifacts demand different principle emphasis. **But every artifact demands THE DARE.**

### Web UI / Landing Page
- Hierarchy and Silhouette highest priority.
- Scanning pattern alignment (F vs Z) critical.
- Fitts's Law applies directly to CTA sizing/placement.
- It Factor normally weighted 1.2x.
- **DARE Direction:** Spatial computing, generative heroes, adaptive layouts, scroll-responsive systems.

### Dashboard / Dev Tool / Technical UI
- Restraint and Rhythm matter more than It Factor.
- Hick's Law and Miller's Law are critical.
- Fitts's Law: small frequent actions need large targets.
- Reduce It Factor weight to 0.8x mentally. Increase Restraint to 1.2x.
- **BUT:** Even dashboards need THE DARE. A dashboard that is purely functional is a commodity. A dashboard that includes one generative data visualization, one spatial layer, or one narrative moment is a product.
- **DARE Direction:** Generative data storytelling, spatial data layers, ambient status indicators, narrative anomaly detection.

### Mobile App
- Fitts's Law is paramount — touch targets minimum 44×44pt.
- Hierarchy must work at small scale — single primary per screen.
- Rhythm uses 4pt fine grid for tight spaces.
- Contrast must survive outdoor viewing.
- **DARE Direction:** Haptic-visual feedback, ambient-adaptive interfaces, one-handed spatial gestures, micro-interactions with personality.

### Presentation / Slide Deck
- Silhouette = template consistency across all slides.
- Hierarchy = one idea per slide rule (Miller's Law per slide).
- Rhythm = consistent margins and type sizes across slides.
- Peak-End Rule applies: opening slide (peak) and closing slide (end) matter most.
- **DARE Direction:** One slide that breaks the template completely (McQueen moment), one generative/live data slide, one spatial depth slide.

### Brand System (Logo, Type, Palette)
- Treat as single cohesive artifact.
- Silhouette = logo shape distinctiveness and scalability.
- It Factor = brand asset distinctiveness (Von Restorff applied to competitive landscape).
- Contrast = palette usability across applications.
- Rhythm = system flexibility.
- **DARE Direction:** One future-cast brand asset (generative logo, adaptive type system, chameleon palette), one polarizing brand choice that makes 50% of stakeholders uncomfortable.

### Image / Photo / Physical Object (No Code Access)
- Assume presented visual is intentional. Audit what you see.
- Apply all principles visually: Figure/Ground, visual weight, balance, color harmony.
- Flag inability to measure WCAG ratios without text layer access.
- Note assumptions explicitly in report.
- Focus on It Factor, Silhouette, and Hierarchy as primary pillars.
- **DARE Direction:** One material/finish/texture that doesn't belong in the category. One scale shift that violates expectation. One narrative element that tells a story beyond the object.
