## PROTAGONIST DIAGNOSIS

**Who is the hero?** The product is the hero. The reader is the audience. This is fatal.

**Evidence:** "Our platform streamlines workflow automation..." — The first sentence establishes the product as the protagonist.

**What do they want?** Abstract: "better productivity." Not specific, not emotional, not personal.

**What do they fear?** Unnamed. No fear is acknowledged. The text operates as if the reader is already convinced.

**What contradiction drives them?** None. No internal conflict. The text assumes a rational optimizer, not a human with competing desires.

**Protagonist Score: 12/100** — The reader is not the protagonist. They are a spectator.

## ANTAGONIST DIAGNOSIS

**Who/what opposes the protagonist?** "Manual processes" and "inefficient workflows."

**Is the antagonist internal or external?** External and generic. "Inefficiency" is not an antagonist. It is a consultant's buzzword.

**Does naming the antagonist make the reader uncomfortable?** No. The reader nods politely. There is no discomfort because there is no recognition.

**Strong antagonist would be:** "The 4pm panic when you realize the report is due tomorrow and the data is in three different tools and none of them talk to each other and you have to explain to your director why you missed the deadline again. The shame of being competent at everything except the one thing that determines your promotion."

**Antagonist Score: 8/100** — No internal opposition. No personal stake. The enemy is a PowerPoint slide.

## INCITING INCIDENT REPORT

**Location:** Paragraph 3, sentence 2. "In today's fast-paced business environment..."

**Strength:** Generic. "Today's fast-paced business environment" is not an inciting incident. It is wallpaper.

**Irreversibility:** The reader can finish this sentence, close the tab, and continue their day unchanged.

**Force Rating: 5/100** — There is no specific moment. No specific person. No specific consequence. The inciting incident is a cliché dressed as insight.

**Strong inciting incident would be:** "Last Tuesday at 2:47pm, Sarah's automation script failed. She had 3 hours to reconcile Q3 data across Salesforce, Stripe, and a Google Sheet her predecessor built. She missed her kid's recital. She didn't tell anyone. But she started looking for a new job that night."

## SHAPE MAPPING

**Detected Shape:** NONE. Flatline.

**Evidence:** The text reads: Problem → Feature → Benefit → Feature → Benefit → CTA. There is no emotional curve. No rise and fall. No transformation. It is a list in prose form.

**Vonnegut diagnosis:** This is a table of contents masquerading as a story. The protagonist's fortune does not move. The Y-axis is flat.

**Recommended Shape:** Man in a Hole. The product saves time. The journey is: bearable life → crisis → recovery → better than before. This is the natural shape for a workflow product.

**Shape Score: 0/100** — No detectable emotional arc.

## THE SURGERY (Ranked by Narrative Impact)

### 1. REWRITE THE PROTAGONIST (Effort: `rebuild`)
**Current State:** The product is the hero.
**The Wound:** McKee's principle: The protagonist pursues desire through conflict. If there is no protagonist, there is no story.
**The Cut:** Rewrite from the reader's perspective. Every sentence must make the reader feel something about their own life.
**Before:** "Our platform streamlines workflow automation for enterprise teams."
**After:** "You check Slack at 11pm not because you're dedicated. Because you're terrified tomorrow's standup will expose that you still don't have the numbers."
**Expected Effect:** Protagonist Score: 12 → 75. Narrative Vitality: +35 points.

### 2. ENGINEER THE INCITING INCIDENT (Effort: `1hr`)
**Current State:** "In today's fast-paced business environment..."
**The Wound:** The inciting incident must be specific, visceral, and irreversible. Generic contexts are not incidents.
**The Cut:** Replace with a specific moment. Name a person. Name a time. Name a consequence.
**Before:** "In today's fast-paced business environment, teams need to move faster."
**After:** "Last Tuesday at 2:47pm, Maria's macro broke. She had 3 hours to reconcile Q3 data across Salesforce, Stripe, and a Google Sheet her predecessor built in 2019. She missed her kid's recital. She started looking for a new job that night."
**Expected Effect:** Inciting Incident Force: 5 → 85. Narrative Vitality: +25 points.

### 3. NAMING THE ANTAGONIST (Effort: `half-day`)
**Current State:** "Manual processes" and "inefficient workflows."
**The Wound:** External, generic antagonists create no emotional investment. The antagonist must be internal and personal.
**The Cut:** Name the fear. Name the shame. Name the self-deception.
**Before:** "Stop wasting time on manual processes."
**After:** "Your current workflow is not broken. It is good enough — and good enough is the most expensive thing you own. It works just well enough that you never fix it, and just poorly enough that you never thrive. The real enemy is not inefficiency. It's the comfortable lie that 'this is just how it is.'"
**Expected Effect:** Antagonist Score: 8 → 78. Narrative Vitality: +20 points.

### 4. INJECT THE ORDEAL (Effort: `architectural`)
**Current State:** No struggle. No near-death. No customer story of vulnerability.
**The Wound:** Vogler's stage 8: The Ordeal is where transformation happens. A frictionless journey is a slide, not a story.
**The Cut:** Add a specific customer story of failure, panic, and recovery.
**Before:** "Easy setup. Start in minutes."
**After:** "Our biggest customer's data corrupted during migration. They threatened to churn. Our engineer spent 48 hours rebuilding their workflow. They didn't just stay. They tripled their contract. The setup is not easy. The setup is worth it."
**Expected Effect:** Hero's Journey Completion: 4/12 → 9/12. Narrative Vitality: +18 points.

### 5. CONSTRUCT THE SPARKLINE (Effort: `half-day`)
**Current State:** No oscillation between reality and potential.
**The Wound:** Duarte's sparkline: persuasion requires alternating between "what is" and "what could be," widening the gap each cycle.
**The Cut:** Restructure the body into ≥3 oscillations.
**Example:**
> "Your team uses spreadsheets. They work. Everyone knows where to find them. [Reality]
>
> But last Tuesday, Sarah spent 3 hours reconciling version 4.2 with version 4.2_final and version 4.2_final_ACTUAL. She missed her kid's recital. [Deeper reality]
>
> Imagine Sarah walking out at 5pm because the reconciliation took 4 minutes. Imagine her recommending your company to friends because 'they actually respect my time.' [Potential]
>
> Your current system is not broken. It is 'good enough.' And 'good enough' is the most expensive thing you own. [Deeper reality still]
>
> The competitor who replaces you will not have a better product. They will have a better Sarah experience. And Sarah talks to other Sarahs. [Potential — competitive consequence]
>
> **Start the transformation.** Not with a tool. With a protocol that changes how your team sees their own potential."
**Expected Effect:** Sparkline Oscillations: 0 → 3. Narrative Vitality: +15 points.

### 6. REDESIGN THE CLIMAX (Effort: `1hr`)
**Current State:** "Start your free trial" — generic, predictable, resistible.
**The Wound:** McKee: The climax is the most meaningful scene. It must feel inevitable and irreversible.
**The Cut:** Make the CTA the only logical outcome of the narrative arc. Name the transformation, not the action.
**Before:** "Start your free trial"
**After:** "Claim your protocol. Not a tool. A constraint that liberates. The setup takes 20 minutes. The transformation takes 20 days. Start tonight — before tomorrow's 11pm Slack check."
**Expected Effect:** Climax Inevitability: 10 → 72. Narrative Vitality: +12 points.
