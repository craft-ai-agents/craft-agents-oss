# ARC-HER — Tactical Frameworks

Reference document for the operational playbooks used by the `arc-her` skill.

## 1. Shape Selection Guide

### Shape-Product Alignment Matrix

| Product Type | Best Shape | Why | Second-Best |
|--------------|-----------|-----|-------------|
| **Security / Compliance** | Kafka's Metamorphosis | Permanent threat, no recovery | Old Testament (Riches to Rags) |
| **Lifestyle / Wellness** | Cinderella | Transformation through reversal | New Testament (Rags to Riches) |
| **Productivity / SaaS** | Man in a Hole | Problem → Solution → Better than before | Boy Meets Girl (loss → return) |
| **Community / Social** | Hamlet (Staircase) | Oscillating engagement, unresolved | Boy Meets Girl |
| **Education / Self-Improvement** | New Testament | Transformation journey | Cinderella |
| **Creative Tool / Design** | Which Way Is Up? | Ambiguity as aesthetic | Hamlet |
| **E-commerce / Luxury** | Cinderella | Gift → reversal → transcendent | Boy Meets Girl |
| **B2B Enterprise** | Man in a Hole | Problem → operational excellence | Old Testament (warning tale) |

### Shape Mixing Protocol
Multi-section texts (landing pages, email sequences) can use different shapes for different sections:

**Landing Page Architecture:**
- **Hero:** Man in a Hole (quick problem → promise)
- **Problem Section:** Kafka's Metamorphosis (permanent threat)
- **Solution Section:** New Testament (transformation)
- **Testimonial:** Cinderella (deep struggle → gift → reversal → triumph)
- **CTA:** Boy Meets Girl (loss → return, "don't lose this")

**Email Sequence Architecture (5 emails):**
- Email 1: Kafka (the world has changed irreversibly)
- Email 2: Man in a Hole (you have a problem, here's how deep)
- Email 3: Hamlet (the data keeps changing — stay tuned)
- Email 4: Cinderella (a customer's story of reversal and triumph)
- Email 5: Boy Meets Girl (your trial expires tomorrow — don't lose what you've found)

**Forbidden Combinations:**
- Kafka + Cinderella in the same section = tonal whiplash (dread + redemption without transition)
- Which Way Is Up? + Boy Meets Girl = confusion (ambiguity + clear desire)
- Man in a Hole + Old Testament in the same section = mixed message (recovery + permanent loss)

## 2. Hero's Journey Stage Injections

When a stage is missing or weak, use these injection templates:

### Stage 3: Refusal of the Call (Most Commonly Missing)
**Injection template:** "You're not [lazy/stupid/stuck]. You're [legitimately exhausted/sensibly skeptical/appropriately cautious]. [Specific common objection] is not an excuse — it's a rational response to [specific past experience with bad solutions]."

**Strong example:** "You're not avoiding new tools. You've been burned by three 'all-in-one' platforms that took 6 months to implement and 2 days to abandon. Skepticism is not resistance. It's intelligence."

**Weak example:** "We know change is hard." (Generic, dismissive)

### Stage 7: Approach to Inmost Cave (Second Most Commonly Missing)
**Injection template:** "Here's the part nobody talks about: [specific implementation difficulty]. [Specific fear]. [Specific moment of doubt]. This is where 60% of [customer type] quit. Not because the tool is wrong. Because the transition feels like [specific metaphor]."

**Strong example:** "Here's the part nobody talks about: importing your data takes 4 hours. Your team will ask 'why are we switching?' three times. Someone will say 'the old way was fine.' This is where 60% of teams quit. Not because the tool is wrong. Because the transition feels like moving houses while still living in the old one."

**Weak example:** "Implementation is easy with our support team." (Denies the ordeal)

### Stage 8: The Ordeal (Third Most Commonly Missing)
**Injection template:** "[Specific worst moment]. [Specific consequence]. [Specific emotional response]. Then [specific turning point]."

**Strong example:** "Our biggest customer's data corrupted during migration. They threatened to sue. Our CEO didn't sleep for 48 hours. Then our engineer found the backup protocol we'd built for exactly this. The customer didn't just stay. They tripled their contract."

**Weak example:** "We had some challenges but our team handled them." (Vague, sanitized)

### Stage 10: The Road Back (Fourth Most Commonly Missing)
**Injection template:** "Going back is possible. [Specific old habit that tries to return]. [Specific temptation to revert]. But now you know [specific insight that makes returning impossible]."

**Strong example:** "You can go back to spreadsheets. Your old templates are still there. The team won't complain — they'll just quietly revert. But now you know what 'real-time visibility' means. You've seen the dashboard update while you're still in the meeting. Going back feels like driving a horse after flying."

**Weak example:** "You'll never want to go back." (Unsubstantiated assertion)

## 3. Sparkline Construction Manual

### The Oscillation Formula
Each oscillation consists of:
1. **Reality Anchor** — Specific, undeniable fact about the reader's current world
2. **Potential Tease** — Specific, sensory glimpse of the transformed world
3. **Return to Reality** — Deeper, more specific version of the current reality (the gap widens)

**Weak oscillation (no gap widening):**
> "You use spreadsheets. [Reality] We have a dashboard. [Potential] You use manual reports. [Reality — same depth] We have automation. [Potential]"

**Strong oscillation (gap widens):**
> "You use spreadsheets. They work. Everyone knows where to find them. [Reality] But last Tuesday, Sarah spent 3 hours reconciling version 4.2 with version 4.2_final and version 4.2_final_ACTUAL. She missed her kid's recital. [Deeper reality]
>
> Imagine Sarah walking out at 5pm because the reconciliation took 4 minutes. Imagine her recommending your company to friends because 'they actually respect my time.' [Potential]
>
> Your current system is not broken. It is 'good enough.' And 'good enough' is the most expensive thing you own. [Deeper reality still]
>
> The competitor who replaces you will not have a better product. They will have a better Sarah experience. And Sarah talks to other Sarahs. [Potential — competitive consequence]"

### The Climax Position
The climax (CTA) must appear at the **widest point of the final gap** — the moment the reader feels the maximum distance between their current reality and the potential reality.

**Wrong placement:** Climax after the first oscillation (gap hasn't widened enough)
**Wrong placement:** Climax after describing the new bliss (reward before commitment)
**Correct placement:** Climax at the peak of the final potential tease, before the reader can mentally resolve the tension.

## 4. Inciting Incident Engineering

### The 3-Sentence Rule
The inciting incident must be felt by sentence 3. If it is not, the reader's attention has already been allocated elsewhere.

### Inciting Incident Templates by Shape

**Man in a Hole:**
> "[Specific time, specific day], [specific person] was [specific normal activity]. Then [specific disruption]. [Specific irreversible consequence]."
> Example: "Last Tuesday at 2:47pm, Maria was updating the Q3 forecast. Then the macro broke. Three hours of work. Gone. She had to call her director and say 'I don't know when I'll have the numbers.'"

**Boy Meets Girl:**
> "[Specific person] didn't know they needed [thing]. Then they [found/lost] it. Now [specific consequence of absence]."
> Example: "Tom didn't know he needed async standups until his team tried them for a week. Then they went back to daily meetings. Now Tom's engineers are interviewing elsewhere."

**Cinderella:**
> "[Specific person] had [specific misfortune]. Then [specific gift/turning point]. Then [specific catastrophic reversal]."
> Example: "We had $847 in the bank. Then a customer prepaid $50K. Then they churned 30 days later when the feature wasn't ready. We missed payroll for the first time."

**Kafka's Metamorphosis:**
> "[Specific normal thing] has [permanently changed]. [Specific consequence]. [Specific thing that no longer works]."
> Example: "The day ChatGPT launched, every knowledge worker's value proposition changed permanently. The person who writes your blog posts now competes with a machine that costs $20/month. Your 'content strategy' just became a commodity."

### Inciting Incident Power Checklist
- [ ] Does it happen at a specific time? (Not "these days" or "lately")
- [ ] Does it happen to a specific person? (Named, or clearly archetyped)
- [ ] Does it create an irreversible change? (The world before and after are different)
- [ ] Does it raise a question the reader must have answered? ("What happened next?" or "How do I avoid this?")
- [ ] Is it emotionally specific, not intellectually abstract? (Feelings, not concepts)

## 5. Climax Design Protocol

### The 3 Climax Archetypes

**1. The Reversal Climax**
The reader discovers that what they believed was wrong, and the true path is the opposite.
> "You don't need more productivity. You need less. You don't need more tools. You need fewer decisions. This isn't a product. It's a constraint that liberates."
> **Best for:** Contrarian products, minimalism, anti-tool tools

**2. The Sacrifice Climax**
The reader must give up something valuable to gain something transcendent.
> "To get this, you have to give up [specific comfort]. Not because it's bad. Because it's incompatible with [specific transformation]. The old workflow dies today. Are you ready?"
> **Best for:** Transformation products, habit-change tools, platform migrations

**3. The Recognition Climax**
The reader realizes they already knew the answer, but couldn't articulate it.
> "You've felt this. In the 6pm meeting that should have been an email. In the 'quick sync' that ate your afternoon. You knew this was wrong. Now you have a name for it. And a way out."
> **Best for:** Problem-awareness products, naming-the-pain solutions, category creators

### Climax Inevitability Test
Remove the climax (CTA) from the text. Give the text to a reader who doesn't know the product. Ask them: "What should happen next?"
- If they say the CTA verbatim → Inevitability is 90-100
- If they say something similar → Inevitability is 70-89
- If they say "I don't know" or something unrelated → Inevitability is <70. Rewrite everything before the climax.

## 6. Antagonist Deep-Dive Matrix

### Antagonist Taxonomy

| Antagonist Type | Specific Example | Generic Example | Strength |
|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Internal Fear** | "The fear that switching tools makes you look incompetent to your team" | "Fear of change" | Strong |
| **Internal Habit** | "The 4pm email check that feels productive but produces nothing" | "Bad habits" | Strong |
| **Internal Identity** | "The belief that 'I'm not a data person'" | "Skill gaps" | Strong |
| **External Status Quo** | "Your current vendor's 2-year contract with 18-month auto-renewal" | "Legacy systems" | Medium |
| **External Competitor** | "Competitor X just hired your former head of sales" | "Market competition" | Weak |
| **External Market Force** | "The 2024 AI commoditization wave" | "Industry disruption" | Weak |
| **Internal Self-Deception** | "The story you tell yourself that 'we're just a startup, we don't need process'" | "Immaturity" | Strong |
| **External Social Pressure** | "Your board asking 'why are you switching again?'" | "Stakeholder resistance" | Medium |

### Antagonist Strengthening Protocol
If the audit detects a weak antagonist, apply this protocol:

**Step 1: Name the internal version**
"Not 'the market is competitive.' 'Your fear that choosing wrong makes you look stupid to the board you just convinced to hire you.'"

**Step 2: Make it personal**
"Not 'change is hard.' 'The 3am wake-up wondering if you should have stayed at your old job.'"

**Step 3: Make it active**
"Not 'habits are resistant.' 'Your current workflow is not passive. It is actively recruiting your team back to the old way every single day.'"

**Step 4: Make it the protagonist's own creation**
"Not 'the world is changing.' 'You built the exact system that is now trapping you. You chose the vendor. You trained the team. You are the architect of your own cage.'"

## 7. Narrative Surgery Prioritization

When Narrative Vitality Score is <70, apply surgeries in this order:

**Priority 1: Protagonist/Antagonist Alignment**
If the product is the hero → Full rewrite. No exceptions.
If the antagonist is external/generic → Rewrite with internal antagonist.

**Priority 2: Inciting Incident Placement**
If not in first 3 sentences → Move or rewrite. No negotiation.

**Priority 3: Shape Selection**
If no dominant shape → Select appropriate shape and restructure.
If wrong shape for product → Reshape completely.

**Priority 4: Hero's Journey Completion**
If <50% completion → Inject missing stages using templates above.
If missing Refusal, Ordeal, or Road Back → These are non-negotiable. Add them.

**Priority 5: Sparkline Oscillation**
If <2 oscillations → Add at least one more cycle.
If flatline sections → Rewrite to create reality→potential movement.

**Priority 6: Climax Inevitability**
If inevitability <70 → Rewrite the 3 paragraphs before climax.
If CTA is generic → Apply Climax Design Protocol.

**Priority 7: Gap Density**
If no expectation subversions → Add 1-2 sentences that reverse reader's assumption.
If predictability >80 → Restructure at least one section for surprise.

## 8. Domain-Specific Narrative Templates

### SaaS Landing Page (Man in a Hole → New Testament)
**Hero:** Inciting incident (3 sentences) + protagonist desire + antagonist
**Problem Deep-Dive:** Kafka's Metamorphosis (the world has changed permanently)
**Solution Preview:** New Testament (the transformation arc)
**Testimonial:** Cinderella (deep struggle → reversal → triumph)
**Social Proof:** Hamlet (oscillating data, unresolved tension)
**CTA:** Boy Meets Girl (loss → return — "don't lose what you've found")

### Email Sequence (5 emails)
**Email 1 — The Disruption:** Kafka's Metamorphosis. World changed. No going back.
**Email 2 — The Hole:** Man in a Hole. Specific problem. Specific depth.
**Email 3 — The Ordeal:** Cinderella. A customer's real struggle and real reversal.
**Email 4 — The Transformation:** New Testament. The journey from broken to transcendent.
**Email 5 — The Loss:** Boy Meets Girl. Trial ending. Specific thing they'll lose.

### Pitch Deck (10 slides)
**Slide 1 — Ordinary World:** The current reality. Quiet dissatisfactions.
**Slide 2 — Call to Adventure:** The inciting incident. The market shift.
**Slide 3 — Refusal:** Acknowledge skepticism. "You've heard this before."
**Slide 4 — Meeting the Mentor:** Your team's earned authority. Why you.
**Slide 5 — Threshold:** The product. The commitment. The demo.
**Slide 6 — Tests:** Traction. Challenges overcome. Early wins.
**Slide 7 — Approach:** The market gap. The specific fear investors have.
**Slide 8 — Ordeal:** The near-death experience. The pivot. The moment of truth.
**Slide 9 — Reward:** The traction. The growth. The validation.
**Slide 10 — Return with Elixir:** The ask. The return. The transformed world.

### About Page (Cinderella or New Testament)
**Founder Origin:** Specific low point. Specific gift/turning point. Specific reversal. Specific triumph.
**Company Story:** Not "we started in a garage." "We had $200 and 47 days of runway. Then [specific turning point]. Then [specific catastrophe]. Then [specific resurrection]."

### Product Demo Script (Hamlet → Man in a Hole)
**Opening:** Hamlet. "You think this is a [category]. It's actually a [different category]."
**Problem:** Man in a Hole. "Let me show you what happens when [specific scenario]."
**Solution:** Man in a Hole recovery. "Now watch what happens when we apply [feature]."
**Climax:** Recognition. "You've been trying to solve this with [wrong approach]. The answer was always [correct approach]."

---

These frameworks are not suggestions. They are constraints. Work within them, leverage them deliberately, and cite them when auditing. The `arc-her` skill applies them as first-class tools, not decoration.
