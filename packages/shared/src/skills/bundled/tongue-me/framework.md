# TONGUE-ME — Tactical Frameworks

Reference document for the playbooks used by the `tongue-me` skill.

## 1. The Hedge-Word Elimination Matrix

Hedging is the single largest failure mode in marketing copy. Every hedge transmits "I am not sure," and the reader's brain demotes truth-value accordingly.

### The Banned Inventory

| Category | Banned phrase | Sovereign replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion-hedges | "I think," "I believe," "I feel" | Drop entirely. State the claim. |
| Possibility-hedges | "Might," "maybe," "perhaps," "possibly" | Drop. Or replace with "will," "does." |
| Magnitude-hedges | "Sort of," "kind of," "a bit," "somewhat" | Drop. Use the precise term or none. |
| Speech-act-hedges | "Just," "actually," "basically," "literally" | Drop entirely. |
| Frame-hedges | "In my opinion," "from my perspective," "in my view" | Drop. The reader knows whose view it is. |
| Conditional-hedges | "Could be," "would be," "may be" | Replace with "is" where possible. |

### The Critical-Position Rule

A hedge in the hero, headline, or climax is **disqualifying**. The score caps at 60 regardless of how clean the rest of the copy is. The reader meets the voice in those three positions; a hedge there poisons everything downstream.

### The Hedge-Replacement Test

For every hedge identified, apply the test:
1. Drop the hedge. Does the sentence still work? → drop it.
2. Replace with a stronger claim. Does the brand still believe it? → upgrade.
3. Replace with concrete evidence ("3 of 5 clients reported X"). → specificity beats hedging.

If the hedge is needed because the claim is uncertain, the claim itself is the problem. Find a different claim.

## 2. The Disqualifier Construction Template

The most powerful filtration tool is the explicit disqualifier — the sentence that names who is *not* welcome.

### The 5 Disqualifier Patterns

**Pattern 1: The Direct Refusal**
> "We don't work with [type of customer]."
> "We don't take referrals."
> "We don't accept work under [threshold]."

**Pattern 2: The Conditional Disqualifier**
> "If you need [common request], we are the wrong fit."
> "If your timeline is under [interval], we cannot help you."
> "If you are looking for [common service], you want [other provider]."

**Pattern 3: The Inverted Qualifier**
> "We accept [N] [unit] per [period]."
> "Applications open [interval]. Most are declined."
> "We work with founders/operators/buyers who have already [achievement]."

**Pattern 4: The Wrong-Fit Naming**
> "Wrong fit if [characteristic]."
> "This isn't for you if [criterion]."
> "Most teams that ask for [X] are not the teams we serve."

**Pattern 5: The Status Threshold**
> "Past [milestone] only."
> "Reserved for [tier] customers."
> "By introduction or application."

### The Disqualifier Density Rule

For sovereign voice in marketing copy, **at least one disqualifier in the first 100 words**. Two if the brand can sustain it. Three is luxury-tier (Hermès, Soho House, Berghain).

## 3. The Cadence Percussion Method

Sentence rhythm is half the audit. Even perfect content reads dead if the rhythm is flat.

### The Sentence-Length Sequence Test

Extract the artifact's sentences. Count words per sentence. Plot the sequence.

**Flat (failure):** `12, 14, 13, 12, 15, 14, 13, 12, 14`
**Percussive (success):** `4, 12, 18, 3, 8, 22, 5, 1, 14`

The percussive pattern is what high-status voices produce. Short. Then long. Then very short. Then a single word. Then a complete thought that runs longer than the reader expected.

### The Single-Word Sentence

A single-word sentence is the highest-leverage cadence move available. It interrupts. It punctuates. It demands the reader's attention.

```
The product was good.
Not great.
Good.
That was the problem.
```

Use sparingly. One per artifact section. Overused, single-word sentences become a tic.

### The Strategic Comma Splice

Sovereign voices break grammar deliberately. Comma splices that connect two independent clauses without conjunction create urgency that grammatically-correct prose cannot match.

```
"We don't take new clients on referral, the application is the only door."
"This isn't for everyone, that's the point."
```

## 5. The Mystique Calibration Guide

Strategic withholding is a precise craft. Too little = over-explanation; too much = incoherence.

### The Three Things to Withhold

**1. The Mechanism**
Tell the reader what changes, not how it changes.
- Weak: "Our 12-step proprietary methodology starts with discovery and ends with optimization…"
- Strong: "We work in a way that other firms cannot replicate. Apply to find out."

**2. The Inventory**
Tell the reader what fits, not the entire catalog.
- Weak: "We offer [list of 8 services with descriptions]…"
- Strong: "We do three things very well. The list is in the application."

**3. The Roster**
Tell the reader the calibre of the room, not its contents.
- Weak: "Our clients include [list of 12 logos]."
- Strong: "Our roster is private. References on request, after qualification."

### The Anti-Mystique Markers (Auto-Flagged)

- "What this means is…"
- "In other words…"
- "Let me explain…"
- "To put it simply…"
- "The way this works is…"

These are the prose moves of someone who fears being misunderstood. Sovereign voice does not fear misunderstanding. The reader who doesn't get it isn't the audience.

## 6. The Status Inversion Construction Guide

How to write the three-move sequence that defines sovereign voice.

### Move 1: The Refusal (1-2 sentences)

Open by refusing something the reader expected to receive.

**Templates:**
- "We don't [common thing the category does]."
- "We are not [thing the reader expected this to be]."
- "This is not [common framing] for [common audience]."

**Examples:**
- "We don't pitch."
- "This is not a workshop."
- "We are not a creative agency."
- "We don't take referrals."

### Move 2: The Filter (2-4 sentences)

Define who is and isn't welcome, with specificity.

**Templates:**
- "We work with [specific tier]."
- "Most [common audience type] are wrong fits. The right fit is [criterion]."
- "[Specific qualification] is non-negotiable. Without it, we decline."

**Examples:**
- "We work with founders past Series B who have already shipped a brand once."
- "Most agencies pitch us. We decline 90% of inbound. The exception is [criterion]."
- "Past $5M ARR. Founder-led. No agency intermediaries."

### Move 3: The Application (1-2 sentences)

Invert the buyer/seller relationship. Make the reader the candidate.

**Templates:**
- "If you meet [criteria], apply."
- "Access is by [mechanism]. Most candidates are declined."
- "[Action] is the only door."

**Examples:**
- "If this sounds like you, apply below."
- "Access is by introduction or written application. The form takes 12 minutes."
- "The application is the only door. Most are declined within 48 hours."

### The Density Rule

All three moves within 100 words = sovereign voice claimed.
Two moves within 100 words = partial inversion (score caps at 75).
One move = decorative inversion (score caps at 60).
Zero moves = no inversion present (score caps at 50, regardless of other pillars).

## 7. Sample Voice Inversions

### Sample 1: B2B SaaS About Page

**Before (pursuing voice):**
> "Welcome to Acme! We're so excited to share our story with you. Our team of passionate experts is dedicated to helping businesses of all sizes streamline their workflows. Whether you're a startup or an enterprise, we'd love to learn more about your challenges and how we can help. Get in touch today!"

**After (sovereign voice):**
> "Acme is not a productivity tool.
>
> We work with operators past $10M ARR who have already failed at workflow software twice. Most have specific scars: shadow tools, abandoned implementations, the workflow doc no one reads.
>
> If you have those scars, the application takes 8 minutes."

**Score change:** 22 → 87.

### Sample 2: Personal Brand Bio

**Before (pursuing voice):**
> "Hi! I'm a passionate designer and creative thinker who loves helping brands tell their stories. With over 8 years of experience, I bring fresh perspectives to every project. I'm always excited to collaborate with new clients! Let's create something amazing together."

**After (sovereign voice):**
> "I take three brand projects a year.
>
> I take them when the founder has already worked with two other agencies and arrived with the receipts. I do not take projects that have not failed once.
>
> Inquiries by application. Most are declined the same week."

**Score change:** 18 → 91.

### Sample 3: Luxury Member Club

**Before (mid-tier, mass-market voice):**
> "Join our exclusive community of like-minded professionals! Enjoy access to premium events, networking opportunities, and curated experiences. Membership is open to anyone passionate about success. Apply today!"

**After (sovereign voice):**
> "Membership is by introduction or application.
>
> The room is curated. The criteria are private. The decision is binary and quick.
>
> If you have read this far, you may already know whether you belong."

**Score change:** 38 → 94.
