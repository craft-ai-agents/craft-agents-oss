# FULL DRAG — Tactical Frameworks

Reference document for the operational playbooks used by the `full-drag` skill.

## 1. Sacred Cow Hunting Playbook

The sacred cow is a belief so universally held that questioning it feels like heresy. These are the most valuable targets because they have the largest gap between "certainty" and "evidence."

### The 4 Criteria (Mandatory Filter)
Before any stance is approved for public delivery, it must pass all four:

1. **Resonance & Legs:** Does the topic matter to the target audience? Will they feel personally implicated? A contrarian stance on a niche technical specification will not travel. A contrarian stance on a belief that shapes daily behavior (productivity, dating, health, money) will.
2. **One-Sided Consensus:** Is the dominant view so universal that it feels like common sense? If there is already a robust debate with two established sides, there is no sacred cow. The target is the belief that *everyone* repeats without examination.
3. **Genuine Disagreement:** Do you *actually* believe the contrarian view, with evidence or lived experience? Performative contrarianism is detected instantly. The stance must be rigorously defensible from your own knowledge base.
4. **Unique Perspective / Data:** Can you offer something others cannot replicate? Lived experience in the domain, internal data, historical analogy, first-principles reasoning. If 1000 people could have written the same take, it is not a sacred cow slaughter. It is a pile-on.

### Sacred Cow Grading
| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| A | Universal, emotionally defended, zero tolerance for dissent. High stakes. Massive audience. |
| B | Widely held, mildly defended, some dissent exists but is dismissed. Good target. |
| C | Held by majority but already contested. Contrarian take will feel familiar, not shocking. |
| D | Fringe consensus. Small audience. Low distribution potential. |
| F | Already debated to death. No sacred cow left to slaughter. Pick another domain. |

### Hunting Methods
1. **The Template Inversion:** List the top 10 pieces of advice in a domain. Invert each one. Which inversion is actually true? ("Drink 8 glasses of water a day" → "Hydration needs are individual; forced overhydration dilutes electrolytes.")
2. **The Credential Gap:** Find a domain where the most credentialed people disagree with the most popular advice. The gap between expert consensus and public consensus is a sacred cow.
3. **The Outlier Study:** Find one peer-reviewed study, historical event, or lived experience that contradicts the dominant narrative. If the study is sound but ignored, the sacred cow is built on willful blindness.
4. **The Time Travel Test:** Would this advice have been laughed at 50 years ago? Will it be laughed at 50 years from now? If yes, it is a period piece masquerading as eternal truth.
5. **The Incentive Audit:** Who benefits from this belief being true? If the answer is "the people who sell the solution," the sacred cow is a marketing department.

## 2. The Defense Matrix Builder

You do not win by being unattackable. You win by making every attack strengthen your position. The Defense Matrix turns rebuttals into marketing.

### The Anatomy of a Pre-Baked Rebuttal
Every rebuttal has three moves:

1. **The Acknowledgment (1 sentence):** Validate the concern without surrendering. "That's a fair point — a lot of people do mistake contrarianism for trolling." This disarms. It shows you are not reactive. You have already thought about this.
2. **The Pivot (1-2 sentences):** Redirect the attack into support for your stance. "But circadian rhythm research isn't a performance. It's NIH-funded biology. I'm not challenging their work ethic. I'm challenging their universal applicability." The attack is reframed as a misunderstanding of your actual claim.
3. **The Close (1 sentence):** Return to dead-pan. Close the door. Do not invite a follow-up. "If the science is controversial, the controversy is in the culture, not the claim." Full stop.

### The 5 Universal Attacks (and Their Skeletons)

**Attack 1: "You're just being controversial for attention."**
- Acknowledge: Fair heuristic. Many do perform contrarianism for clicks.
- Pivot: But [your evidence type: science / data / lived experience] isn't a performance. I'm not challenging [their identity]. I'm challenging [the structure / assumption].
- Close: If [the evidence] is controversial, the controversy is in the culture, not the claim.

**Attack 2: "Everyone else disagrees with you."**
- Acknowledge: They do. That's precisely why this needs to be said.
- Pivot: Consensus is not evidence. The majority once believed [historical false consensus]. The majority of [this culture] is built by [the in-group] for [the in-group]. The bias is structural.
- Close: I'm not asking for majority approval. I'm pointing out the architecture.

**Attack 3: "That's offensive / insensitive / inappropriate."**
- Acknowledge: [The specific group] are not the enemy. Their experience is valid.
- Pivot: The offense is in the implication that [the out-group] is [negative label]. I'm reversing that implication, not creating a new hierarchy.
- Close: The goal is not to shame [in-group]. It's to stop shaming everyone else.

**Attack 4: "You don't have the credentials / experience to say that."**
- Acknowledge: Credentials are a proxy for expertise, not expertise itself.
- Pivot: But [credential holders in this domain] actually agree with this position — they just don't say it publicly because [structural incentive: funding, tenure, clientele, reputation]. I'm saying what they can't.
- Close: If the argument requires a credential to refute, the credential is doing the work the evidence should do.

**Attack 5: "What about [edge case / exception / counter-example]?"**
- Acknowledge: That's a real phenomenon. It doesn't invalidate the pattern.
- Pivot: Edge cases are useful for refining the model, not for destroying it. The existence of a [counter-example] doesn't mean the [dominant structure] is working for the majority.
- Close: I'm describing the architecture. You're describing the exception. Both can be true.

### Defense Readiness Grading
| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Locked | All 5 attacks have pre-baked rebuttals. Ready to publish. |
| Partial | 3-4 attacks covered. One vulnerability remains. Do not publish until closed. |
| Vulnerable | <3 attacks covered. Publishing now is inviting a pile-on you cannot control. |

## 3. Platform Adaptation Guides

Each platform has a native culture. The stance is constant. The delivery adapts.

### X / Twitter
- **Length:** 280 chars for the standalone, 3-5 tweets for the thread.
- **Structure:** Hook (tweet 1, standalone strong) → Deconstruction (tweet 2) → Proof / CTA (tweet 3). Each tweet must stand alone if detached from the thread.
- **Tone:** Punchy. No jargon. One short sentence per line break. The dead-pan is in the brevity.
- **Engagement tactic:** End with a question that forces a binary response. "Agree or delusional?" "Team early bird or team night owl?"

### LinkedIn
- **Length:** 150-250 words.
- **Structure:** Personal anecdote or industry observation (hook) → The contrarian pivot → The framework → The call for discussion.
- **Tone:** "Thought leadership" dead-pan. Professional but not corporate. The confidence is in the specificity.
- **Engagement tactic:** Ask for experiences, not opinions. "What's your actual peak performance window?" gets more comments than "Do you agree?"

### TikTok / Short-Form Video
- **Length:** 30-60 seconds.
- **Structure:** Visual hook (text on screen: the stance) → 15 seconds of deconstruction (talking head, calm) → 10 seconds of proof (B-roll, data visualization, or gesture) → 5 seconds of CTA ("Drop a 🦉 if you're a night owl").
- **Tone:** Energy 5/10. Slightly more animated than written dead-pan, but still controlled. The camera is close. The eye contact is unbroken.
- **Engagement tactic:** Use a visual prop or background element that telegraphs the stance instantly.

### Dinner Table / Casual Conversation
- **Length:** 30-60 seconds.
- **Structure:** The drop (the stance, stated mid-conversation as if it's common knowledge) → The pause (let the shock land) → The chill elaboration (one sentence of logic) → The deflection (change topic slightly, forcing them to chase).
- **Tone:** 2/10 energy. Almost bored. The most powerful delivery is the one that makes the listener feel like *they* are overreacting.
- **Engagement tactic:** Do not argue. State and move on. The unresolved tension is the hook. They will bring it up again.

### Podcast / Long-Form
- **Length:** 3-5 minute monologue, or woven into a 20-minute interview.
- **Structure:** Setup (the consensus, described fairly) → The turn ("But here's what nobody is saying...") → The thesis (the stance, fully argued) → The defense (pre-bake one rebuttal into the monologue) → The close (return to the bigger picture).
- **Tone:** 4/10 energy. Conversational but precise. The host is a peer. The audience is intelligent. Do not dumb it down.

## 4. The Conversion Funnel Sequencer

Each stage requires a specific psychological trigger. Map them explicitly for every stance.

### Stage 1: Attention
- **Trigger:** Violation of expectation. The stance must break a pattern the brain has learned to ignore.
- **Test:** If a reader can scroll past without a micro-pause, the hook is too soft.
- **Tactics:** Direct contradiction of a universal belief. Inversion of a common aphorism. A statistical anomaly framed as normal.

### Stage 2: Intrigue
- **Trigger:** The feeling of "I never thought about it that way." The reader must feel smart for following your logic.
- **Test:** Can the deconstruction be summarized in one sentence that makes the listener want to hear the next sentence?
- **Tactics:** Show the structural flaw in the consensus (not the people, the architecture). Use analogies from other domains. Make the invisible visible.

### Stage 3: Conviction
- **Trigger:** The "obvious in hindsight" framework. The new model must feel simpler and more elegant than the old one.
- **Test:** If the reader explains your stance to a friend, do they sound knowledgeable? If yes, the framework is sticky.
- **Tactics:** Name the framework. Give it a memorable label. "Chronotype Privilege." "The Incentive Audit." "Credential Inflation." Named ideas spread.

### Stage 4: Adoption
- **Trigger:** Self-interest. The reader must see a personal benefit in adopting the stance.
- **Test:** Does the reader think "This explains why I've always felt [X]" or "This would have saved me [Y]"? If yes, adoption is likely.
- **Tactics:** Connect the stance to a personal pain point the consensus creates. Night-owl shame. Imposter syndrome from credential gaps. Burnout from productivity theater.

### Stage 5: Advocacy
- **Trigger:** Public commitment. The reader must take a small public action that aligns them with the stance.
- **Test:** Is the CTA shareable? Does it invite a binary choice? Does it make the responder feel like they are joining a team?
- **Tactics:** "Share if you agree." No — "Drop your real peak hour. Not your shame hour. Your real one." The response is identity, not agreement.

### Stage 6: Idolization
- **Trigger:** Consistency over time. One contrarian stance is a hot take. Five rigorously defended contrarian stances in different domains is a system. Systems attract disciples.
- **Test:** Can the audience predict your next stance in a new domain based on your principles? If yes, you are a system.
- **Tactics:** Repeat the framework names across stances. Show that the same underlying logic applies to fitness, finance, dating, and design. The consistency is the charisma.

## 5. The Risk & Reward Ledger

Every stance has costs. You do not pretend they don't exist. You calculate them and proceed anyway.

### What You Lose
- **Audience exodus:** The 30-50% who held the consensus belief and will unfollow, unsubscribe, or disengage. This is not a bug. It is the filter.
- **Opportunity cost:** Brands, employers, or collaborators who cannot publicly associate with the stance. You are choosing alignment over access.
- **Emotional labor:** The volume of rebuttals, DMs, and comments increases. Even with a locked Defense Matrix, the attention cost is real.
- **The Boy Who Cried Wolf:** If your contrarianism is performative or under-researched even once, your authority resets to zero.

### What You Gain
- **Attention arbitrage:** In an algorithmic marketplace that rewards polarity, you have created a high-yield asset. Every share, every quote-dunk, every screenshot is distribution.
- **Authority density:** The 30-50% who remain are not casual followers. They are adherents. They share, defend, recruit, and buy at rates 5-10× higher than passive audiences.
- **Category-of-one positioning:** You are no longer competing in the consensus space. You have created your own category where you are the default reference point.
- **System recognition:** Over time, the audience begins to see the pattern. You are not a contrarian. You are a model. Models create movements.
